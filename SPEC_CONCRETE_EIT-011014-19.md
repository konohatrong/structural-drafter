# Clause digest

## 1. General (บททั่วไป) — PDF p.21–27

### 1.1 Scope (ขอบเขต)
- [REQ] Applies to ordinary concrete work (งานคอนกรีตธรรมดา), both reinforced and unreinforced (plain).
- [REC] Precast concrete, prestressed concrete and other special concretes need additional special practices not covered by this standard.

### 1.2 General requirements (ข้อกำหนดทั่วไป)
- 1.2.1 [REQ] Concrete construction within the scope of 1.1 shall be built in accordance with this standard.
- 1.2.2 [REQ] Concrete construction shall achieve the performance specified at the design stage.
- 1.2.3 [REQ] An engineer knowledgeable and experienced in concrete construction shall be stationed full-time on site (วิศวกร...ประจำอยู่ที่สถานที่ก่อสร้าง).
- 1.2.4 [REQ] A construction plan and clear documents showing the construction sequence/procedure shall be prepared before concrete work starts.
- [REC] (1) Structural quality depends on material quality and construction quality, hence work per the standard.
- [REC] (2) "Engineer" = a licensed professional engineer (holder of a professional practice licence, ใบอนุญาตประกอบวิชาชีพ).
- [REC] (3) A construction plan normally covers: (a) nature, shape, size, location of the structure; (b) construction programme and sequence; (c) intended use of the structure; (d) materials; (e) methods, tools and plant; (f) formwork, shoring (ค้ำยัน) and scaffolding; (g) temporary facilities (conveyors, haul roads, electricity, water supply, etc.); (h) labour plan; (i) safety measures; (j) testing and quality-control criteria; (k) persons responsible; (l) others.

### 1.3 Concrete quality requirements (ข้อกำหนดคุณภาพคอนกรีต)
General: plant-produced (ready-mixed) or site-mixed concrete should have the following properties at each stage. (Clause wording uses "ควร" = should, but it is the normative clause body.)

#### 1.3.1 Fresh concrete (คอนกรีตสด)
- [REQ] Should have good placeability/workability (ความสามารถในการเทได้) and fill forms well, with acceptable workability (slump) loss. Good placeability means:
  1. placeability/flowability appropriate to the particular construction;
  2. no segregation (การแยกตัว) causing non-uniform concrete quality;
  3. no blocking caused by coarse aggregate accumulating at any part of the formwork or reinforcement during placing.

#### 1.3.2 Concrete in plastic state (คอนกรีตในสถานะพลาสติก)
- [REQ] Should: (1) have no or only slight bleeding (การเยิ้มน้ำ); (2) have no or only slight plastic settlement; (3) have no plastic-shrinkage cracking; (4) be easy to finish.

#### 1.3.3 Early-age concrete (คอนกรีตอายุต้น)
- [REQ] Should: (1) have no or only slight autogenous shrinkage, not causing cracking from restraint stresses due to autogenous shrinkage; (2) have no thermal cracking; (3) have sufficient early-age compressive strength.

#### 1.3.4 Hardened concrete (คอนกรีตที่แข็งตัวแล้ว)
Should have the required long-term properties:
- (1) [REQ] Mechanical properties – compressive strength and modulus of elasticity. Compressive strength sufficient to carry stresses from design loads with an appropriate safety factor; modulus of elasticity not less than the value used in structural design.
- (2) [REQ] Durability (ความคงทน) – depends on exposure environment. Concrete shall be designed with high quality, low permeability and adequate cover to reinforcement for the type and severity of exposure, so it resists deterioration and protects reinforcement from corrosion. Consider:
  - (2.1) Expansion on exposure to water/moisture – expansion (% of initial specimen length) from standard test shall not exceed the specified value within the specified time; must not harm adjacent members.
  - (2.2) Drying shrinkage (การหดตัวแบบแห้ง) – shall not cause cracks visible to the naked eye; shrinkage (microstrain, "ไมครอน") shall not exceed the specified value, which depends on restraint, tensile strength/crack resistance and other properties (e.g., fatigue).
  - (2.3) Carbonation – carbonation depth (mm) of standard concrete by Standard Accelerated Test shall not exceed the specified value; the value is set so that carbonation front does not reach the outermost reinforcement before the designed maintenance-free service life.
  - (2.4) Reinforcement corrosion – concrete should have low permeability to limit ingress of water, gas, solutions and ions; may be assessed by water permeability test; water permeability (cm³/cm²/s) shall not exceed specified value; the limit may vary with cover (greater cover may allow higher permeability). Chloride must also be considered: acid-soluble chloride at the outermost reinforcement shall not exceed the specified value.
  - (2.5) Alkali–aggregate reaction – concrete shall not be at risk of alkali–silica, alkali–silicate or alkali–carbonate reaction. If AAR risk exists, cement alkali content shall not exceed the specified limit; if it does, other binders (e.g., fly ash) should be used to reduce alkalinity.
  - (2.6) Abrasion/erosion (การสึกกร่อน) – concrete should not suffer severe abrasion during the design service life; an abrasion-resistance test method should be used; requirements depend on member/structure type and exposure.
  - (2.7) Sulfate exposure – concrete shall be sulfate resistant; expansion (% of initial length) and/or mass loss from standard sulfate expansion or weight-loss test shall not exceed specified values within the specified time.
  - (2.8) Other chemicals (acids, salts) – % mass loss relative to initial shall not exceed the specified value within the specified time.
  - (2.9) Freeze–thaw – where concrete is used in very cold environments (temperatures reaching freezing), it shall withstand freeze–thaw cycles with loss of modulus of elasticity not exceeding the specified % of the initial value.
  - (2.10) Biological deterioration – % loss of compressive strength relative to initial, by Specified Accelerated Degradation Test, shall not exceed the specified value.
  - (No numerical limits are given in the standard for 2.1–2.10; "specified value" = project-specified.)

Production criteria to avoid undesirable properties:
- (1) [REQ] Cement content / binder content per m³ (ปริมาณปูนซีเมนต์หรือวัสดุประสานต่อ ลบ.ม.) – computed from water content per m³ divided by w/c (or w/b) ratio. Must not be so low that paste is insufficient to fill aggregate voids and bind aggregates (→ porous, poor strength); must not be excessive (→ high heat of hydration, thermal cracking, durability problems such as high shrinkage). For lean/"coarse" concrete (คอนกรีตหยาบ), paste need not fill all voids but must bind aggregates with adequate strength. Pozzolans used in concrete shall be counted as part of the binder (cementitious materials).
- (2) [REQ] Water–cement / water–binder ratio – normally chosen for required strength, but in some exposures a low w/b must be specified even if the resulting strength exceeds the design strength. See Table 1.1.

**Table 1.1 – Maximum water–binder ratio for various service conditions (อัตราส่วนน้ำต่อวัสดุประสานสูงสุด)**

| Concrete condition | Max. w/b |
|---|---|
| 1) Structures requiring watertightness (ความทึบน้ำ) | 0.50 |
| 2) Structures at **moderate** risk of reinforcement corrosion or concrete deterioration | 0.50 |
| 3) Structures at **severe** risk of reinforcement corrosion or concrete deterioration | 0.45 |

- (3) [REQ] Water content per m³ – must not be excessive; in principle only enough for the required workability. Excess water, even with w/b controlled, increases bleeding and reduces durability (high drying shrinkage, low watertightness, etc.).
- (4) [REQ] Chloride content in concrete – acid-soluble chloride permitted in concrete (before any external chloride ingress) shall not exceed Table 1.2.

**Table 1.2 – Maximum chloride ion content for corrosion protection (ปริมาณคลอไรด์ที่ละลายในกรดที่ยอมให้ในส่วนผสมคอนกรีต, % by mass of binder/cementitious material)**

| Type of construction | Max. acid-soluble Cl⁻ (% by wt of binder) |
|---|---|
| 1. Prestressed concrete | 0.08 |
| 2. Reinforced concrete exposed to chloride in service | 0.20 |
| 3. Reinforced concrete that is dry or protected from moisture in service | 1.00 |
| 4. Other reinforced concrete construction | 0.30 |

Note: total acid-soluble chloride determined per ASTM C1152/C1152M (Standard Test Method for Acid-Soluble Chloride in Mortar and Concrete).
- High chloride leads to reinforcement corrosion; sodium chloride also promotes AAR.
- Chloride content at mixing = total chloride ion computed from all constituents (aggregates, chemical admixtures, mixing water, etc.) relative to concrete mass [note: text says "ต่อน้ำหนักของคอนกรีต" here although the table is per binder mass]; excludes chloride penetrating from outside during service.

## 2. Materials (วัสดุ) — PDF p.29–70

### 2.1 Cement (ปูนซีเมนต์)
- 2.1.1 [REQ] Standard cement (ปูนซีเมนต์มาตรฐาน): Portland cement for structural concrete complying with **TIS 15 (มอก. 15) Portland Cement, Part 1 (เล่ม 1)**.
- 2.1.2 [REQ] Mixed cement (ปูนซีเมนต์ผสม): complying with **TIS 80 (มอก. 80) Mixed cement**.
- 2.1.3 [REQ] Other cements:
  1. Pozzolan cement – TIS 849
  2. Refractory mortar/cement (ปูนทนไฟ) – TIS 556
  3. White cement (ปูนซีเมนต์ขาว) – TIS 133
  4. Gypsum plaster for construction (ปูนยิปซั่ม) – TIS 188
  5. Lime (ปูนไลม์) – TIS 202 Parts 1 and 2
  6. Masonry cement (ปูนก่อ) – TIS 598
  7. High-alumina cement (ปูนซีเมนต์อะลูมินา) – TIS 556 (as printed; same number as item 2 – likely a typo in the source)
  8. Any cement complying with a promulgated TIS standard.
- [REC] (1) Portland cement types by use:
  - Type I – general structures and concrete work;
  - Type II – moderate heat of hydration and moderate sulfate resistance;
  - Type III – high early strength, early form stripping, prestressed concrete;
  - Type IV – low heat, e.g., mass concrete (คอนกรีตหลา);
  - Type V – sulfate resistant, e.g., concrete in contact with groundwater or wastewater with high sulfate.
  - Substitution is possible: Type I + chemical admixture for high early strength instead of Type III; partial pozzolan replacement to reduce heat instead of Type IV; pozzolan with Type I for sulfate resistance — but trial mixes/tests must confirm required properties before use.
- [REC] (2) Mixed cement (TIS 80) is for masonry or plastering only; should NOT be used in concrete for main structural members (columns, beams, footings, slabs).
- [REC] (3) Other cements:
  - a. Pozzolan cement – trial mixes and tests comparing properties with requirements before actual use.
  - b. Refractory mortar – for jointing firebricks.
  - c. White cement – decorative only (terrazzo, exposed aggregate/washed finish, ceramic/mosaic tiling); not suitable for main structures/general concrete (lower 28-day strength than Type I, more expensive).
  - d. Gypsum plaster – finish plastering for smooth surfaces; aesthetic, not load-bearing.
  - e. Lime – use per its chemical/physical properties and manufacturer's recommendations (e.g., fluxing lime for steel/glass making; lime mortar for masonry).
  - f. Masonry cement – choose type per TIS 598 Annex A (ภาคผนวก ก) and engineering design of the structure.
  - g. High-alumina cement – good for sulfate resistance, rapid strength, or very high service temperatures (e.g., > 1000 °C). Cautions: at normal to high temperatures (below ~1000 °C), especially humid, strength drops substantially over time (increased paste porosity, i.e., conversion); strength is lower if curing temperature is higher, esp. with high w/c; mixing with other Portland cements tends to cause flash set — use only in suitable work, not in work requiring long-term durability; avoid placing HAC concrete together with fresh Portland-cement concrete and do not store HAC in direct contact with Portland cement.
  - Use of other cements is permitted provided a standard or test results confirm quality.

### 2.2 Mixing water and curing water (น้ำผสมคอนกรีตและน้ำบ่มคอนกรีต)

#### 2.2.1 Mixing water (น้ำผสมคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Shall contain no substances harmful to concrete properties (setting/hardening time, strength, volume change, workability) and shall not cause reinforcement corrosion.
- Potable tap water (drinkable) is normally acceptable.
- Water containing chloride is unsuitable; avoid seawater or brackish water near the sea. If really necessary, seawater may be used only for **unreinforced** concrete; seawater still affects long-term strength and durability.
- Acidic water may harm strength; sugar in water affects setting time — must be strictly avoided.
- Water should be colourless without much suspended matter.
- Acceptance test for questionable water: compare concrete made with the water vs. same mix with clean water:
  - compressive strength ≥ **90 %** of the clean-water concrete;
  - setting time vs clean-water concrete: not earlier by more than **1 h** and not later by more than **1.5 h**.
- Ready-mix plants: truck wash water may be recycled for mixing if proven not harmful; suspended solids, chloride, sulfate and alkali must be controlled not to exceed Table 2.1.

**Table 2.1 – Permissible impurities in mixing water (ppm = mg/L)**

| Substance | Max. permitted (ppm or mg/L) |
|---|---|
| 1) Chloride* – 1.1) prestressed concrete or bridge work | 500 |
| 1) Chloride* – 1.2) general reinforced concrete | 1,000 |
| 2) Sulfate (SO₄) | 3,000 |
| 3) Alkali (Na₂O + 0.658K₂O) | 600 |
| 4) Suspended solids | 50,000 |

\* In any case total chloride in concrete shall not exceed clause 1.3 (Table 1.2).

#### 2.2.2 Curing water (น้ำบ่มคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Need not be as high a standard as mixing water, but shall not be contaminated with oil, acid or salt in amounts harmful to concrete (e.g., attacking the surface).

### 2.3 Aggregates (มวลรวม)
- 2.3.1 [REQ] Fine aggregate (มวลรวมละเอียด): clean, sound/durable, suitably graded; shall comply with **TIS 566 (มอก. 566)**.
- 2.3.2 [REQ] Coarse aggregate (มวลรวมหยาบ): clean, sound/durable, suitably graded; shall comply with **TIS 566**.
- [REC] (1) Aggregate properties and effects:
  - a. Grading – finer grading needs more cement/binder; affects workability, compaction and finishing.
  - b. Deleterious substances: clay lumps and friable particles (reduce workability and abrasion resistance); material finer than 75 µm (No. 200 sieve) (reduces workability, raises water demand → lower strength); coal (reduces compressive and flexural strength; increases chemical admixture demand); lightweight chert (SG < 2.4) (reduces durability, causes popouts); cherts ("chocolate bars") and clayey limestone (swell when wet, shrink when dry, large volume change under freeze–thaw → spalling).
  - c. Mechanical: soundness (affects durability esp. in high-sulfate environments); abrasion resistance – coarse aggregate abrasion loss shall not exceed TIS 566 limits, esp. for impact/abrasion structures (road pavements, factory floors, airfield pavements).
  - d. Organic impurities in fine aggregate affect setting, strength and durability.
- [REC] (2) Alkali–aggregate reaction: some sources (e.g., silica-rich coarse/fine aggregate) react with cement alkalis → expansion and cracking. Check reactivity before use per **ASTM C1260**. If such aggregate must be used, use low-alkali cement (**Na₂O + 0.658K₂O ≤ 0.6 % by mass of cement**) or pozzolan blending, the latter to be tested first to prove it reduces expansion.
- [REC] (3) High-strength concrete or concrete designed for pumping: coarse aggregate flakiness index and elongation index each **≤ 25 %**, tested to **BS 812: Section 105.1 and 105.2** respectively.
- [REC] (4) Sea sand: may be used where fresh (river) sand is unavailable. In reinforced concrete, rebar coating may be used against chloride corrosion; chloride ion must be tested not to exceed **0.02 % by mass of dry sand**, or **0.03 % as NaCl**. Total chloride in concrete shall still not exceed clause 1.3 limits.
- [REC] (5) Crushed/manufactured sand (ทรายย่อย): may replace natural sand if it meets TIS 566. Because it has little fines, grading passing No. 8 (2.36 mm) sieve may be increased by a further **5 %** of total sand, and material passing 75 µm may be up to **≤ 10 %**.
- [REC] (6) Some aggregates cause durability problems esp. AAR, e.g., those containing opal, chalcedony, tridymite, acid or intermediate volcanic glasses — choose aggregate types that avoid long-term durability problems.

#### 2.3.3 Recycled aggregate (มวลรวมที่นำกลับมาใช้ใหม่)

##### 2.3.3.1 Scope
- [REQ] For assessing quality of recycled fine and coarse aggregate from construction, demolition, concrete/test-specimen debris or crushed precast elements, for producing new concrete. Shall be free of harmful contaminants (steel, glass, soft material, tar, asphalt, etc.) in excessive amounts.

### 2.4.2 Chemical admixtures (สารเคมีผสมเพิ่ม) (pp. 52–54)

#### 2.4.2.1 Definition [REQ]
- Chemicals added to concrete (in mixing water before mixing, during mixing, or before placing) to improve properties, e.g. workability, strength, water reduction, resistance, retarding or accelerating hardening.
- Chemical admixtures used in concrete **shall** comply with **มอก. 733** (TIS 733; year not stated).

#### 2.4.2.2 Types [REQ]
| Type | Thai / English | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| A | สารลดน้ำ — Water reducers / plasticizers | Reduces water per unit volume at unchanged placeability |
| B | สารหน่วงการแข็งตัว — Retarders | Lengthens setting time |
| C | สารเร่งการแข็งตัว — Accelerators | Shortens setting time |
| D | Water-reducing and set-retarding | Reduces water at same placeability and lengthens setting time |
| E | Water-reducing and set-accelerating | Reduces water at same placeability and shortens setting time |
| F | High-range water reducer / superplasticizer | Reduces water by **at least 12 %** at same placeability |
| G | High-range water-reducing and set-retarding (set-retarding superplasticizer) | Reduces water by **at least 12 %** and lengthens setting time |
| (8) Others | — | e.g. corrosion inhibitors for reinforcement, waterproofers, air-entraining agents, thickening/viscosity agents such as anti-washout admixtures (underwater), pumping aids |

#### Commentary [REC] (p.54)
- Properly used, admixtures improve workability (via water reduction), watertightness, strength; effectiveness depends on admixture type, cement quality, use of mineral admixtures, aggregate quality and mix proportions.
- **Chloride**: where an admixture contains chloride, dosage should be limited so that chloride contributed by the admixture is **≤ 0.02 % of the mass of binder (cementitious material)**.
- Admixtures with accelerating/retarding effect: control dosage to avoid abnormal setting (possibly severely delayed hardening).
- **All admixtures shall declare their main chemical constituents, instructions for use, and maximum permissible dosage.**
- Using more than one admixture in the same mix: consider interactions; consult manufacturer or run trial mixes.
- No data / new product: run trial mixes and test workability, early and long-term strength, durability before use on site.
- With pozzolans (e.g. fly ash): choose admixture type carefully; set-retarding water reducers (Type D or G) may greatly delay strength development of fly-ash concrete or prevent hardening, especially at high pozzolan and admixture dosages.

### 2.4.3 Other admixtures (สารผสมเพิ่มอื่น ๆ) [REQ] (p.55)
- Examples: pumping aids, anti-washout admixtures, corrosion inhibitors, waterproofers, air-entraining agents.
- Use of these **shall** be preceded by trial mixes to establish the proper dosage and tests on the resulting concrete (workability, early strength, long-term strength, durability) before actual use.
- Although Thailand is not a cold climate, air entrainment may be needed for some works (e.g. cold-storage rooms). Air-entrained concrete should be designed with allowance for the loss of strength and watertightness caused by the entrained air.

### 2.5 Reinforcement (การเสริมความแข็งแรง)

#### 2.5.1 Steel bars for reinforced concrete (เหล็กเส้นเสริมคอนกรีต)

##### 2.5.1.1 Properties [REQ] (p.55)
1. Reinforcing bars **shall** comply with **มอก. 20** (Steel bars for reinforced concrete: round bars, เหล็กเส้นกลม) or **มอก. 24** (Steel bars for reinforced concrete: deformed bars, เหล็กข้ออ้อย).

1. Cut/bend to specified dimensions without damage; use a suitable bending machine where possible. **Re-bending (straightening bent bars) should not be done** as it damages steel. If bent bars must be re-bent for construction joints, bend with the largest radius practicable to limit damage.
2. Bending at welds should be avoided; **bars shall not be bent within 10 db of a weld**.
3. Machine bending at room temperature (cold bending) is the standard. Where heat must be used for hot-rolled bars, heat to **400 °C – 950 °C**; avoid rapid cooling (quenching). Where damage from insufficient heating, water quenching, or the bending method is suspected, perform bend tests first or follow proven practice, especially for field hot-bending.

##### 2.5.1.3 Placing (การวางเหล็ก) [REQ] (p.58)
1. Before placing, reinforcement **should** be cleaned free of dust or any material harmful to bond (แรงยึดหน่วง, bonding) with concrete.
2. Bars **shall** be placed accurately in the specified positions and held securely so they do not displace during concreting. In congested areas, especially at important splices, bars **should** be tied with **soft annealed steel wire 0.9 mm diameter**; if wire is not used, adequate bar holders/fixings should be provided.
3. Spacers (ลูกหนุน) **shall** be placed at suitable intervals to obtain the required cover thickness. **Spacers in contact with formwork should be made of concrete or mortar.**
4. After all reinforcement is placed, it **should** be re-checked for full conformity with the drawings.
5. If concrete is not placed immediately after tying, reinforcement **should** be re-inspected and re-cleaned before concreting.

Commentary [REC] (pp. 58–60):
1. Bars must be free of pitting/flaky rust (สนิมขุม), mud, oil, paint, or other materials.
2. Misplacement may greatly reduce member capacity; cover less than specified reduces durability. Tolerance depends on bar diameter, member importance and direction of reinforcement. Tolerances on effective depth and cover for flexural members, walls and compression members per Table 2.20.

**Table 2.20 — Tolerances on effective depth and cover in RC structures** (p.59, from image)

| Effective depth d | Tolerance on effective depth | Tolerance on cover |
|---|---|---|
| d ≤ 200 mm | ±10 mm | −10 mm |
| d > 200 mm | ±13 mm | −13 mm |

Notes:
1) Distance from the surface of the bottom reinforcement to the bottom (soffit) surface of the member: tolerance not exceeding **−6 mm**.
2) Cover tolerance per Table 2.20, but **not exceeding −1/3 of the cover specified** in the detail drawings.

- Longitudinal location of bends and bar ends: **±50 mm**; except at discontinuous ends of **brackets (แป้นหูช้าง) and corbels (เชิงยื่น): ±13 mm**; at discontinuous ends of other members: **±25 mm**.
- Erection bars help prevent displacement and ease fixing; normally shown on drawings; if not shown, they should still be used in construction.
- At congested intersections use tie wire to hold bars securely; tie wire **not smaller than 0.9 mm** (too-thin wire breaks easily and is not recommended for fixing intersections). Clips or tack (spot) welding may also be used, but **tack welding may damage bars and greatly reduce fatigue strength** due to local heat. Beware damage from unusual loads, sub-standard materials, or bars smaller than specified.
3. Spacers improve accuracy of cover (concrete covering). **Mortar/concrete spacers shall be of better quality than the concrete used.** Avoid many mortar lumps on the tension side of beams (hinder cleaning and concrete flow).
   - **Metal spacers**: experience on RC bridges shows corrosion problems (rust at beam soffits weakens local concrete, may lead to main-bar corrosion) → **metal spacers should not be used where high metal corrosion is expected**.
   - **Plastic spacers require prior approval of the responsible engineer** (different thermal expansion; lower bearing and bond than concrete).
   - **Corrosion-resistant metal spacers (e.g. stainless steel) also require prior approval** of the responsible engineer (dissimilar metals in contact → galvanic corrosion of the anodic metal).
4. After tying, check number and size of bars, positions, **lap lengths**, spacing and bend locations against drawings; preferably inspect progressively during fixing so errors are corrected immediately.
5. If a long time elapses between fixing and concreting, re-check before concreting that bars are still in position and free of mud, oil or other materials.

##### 2.5.1.4 Splices of reinforcing bars (รอยต่อเหล็ก) [REQ] (pp. 60–61)
1. Splicing at locations other than those shown on drawings — both location and method **shall** be approved by the engineer.
2. If splices are not shown on the drawings, splicing **should** follow the splice provisions of the EIT standard for RC buildings by strength design method (**มาตรฐาน วสท. 1008**).
3. Lap splices **shall** have the specified lap length and be tied together with tie wire of diameter **≥ 0.9 mm**.
4. Bars projecting from concrete for future splicing (starter bars) **should** be protected from damage and rust.
5. **Welded splices**: weld strength **shall be ≥ 125 % of the specified yield strength** of the bar. Before starting the steel work, weld strength **shall** be tested by a reliable institution, and **at least 3 copies** of the test results **shall** be submitted to the engineer's office.
6. Splices of adjacent bars **shall not** be in the same line (shall be staggered) and **should be staggered ≥ 1 m**. Bars should not be spliced unless really necessary.
7. **Every splice shall be inspected and approved by the engineer before concreting.** Unapproved splices are deemed defective and may be rejected.
8. **Mechanical splices**: splice strength **shall be ≥ 125 %** of the strength of the reinforcement being spliced (text says "กำลังของเหล็กเสริม" — strength of the bar; cf. 125 % of specified yield for welded splices).

Commentary [REC] (p.61):
1. Splices should comply with **วสท. 1008-38, Chapter 4500 (Splices of reinforcement)**; splices not shown on drawings should comply with this and must not reduce structural strength.
2. **Lap lengths** should comply with **วสท. 1008-38, Chapter 4500**. Soft annealed tie wire along the lap prevents slip; wire should be as short as possible and tied tightly — long wire tails hinder concreting and may reduce bond and lap-splice strength.
3. To protect starter bars left for future splicing: coat with thick cement slurry, or wrap in coal-tar-soaked cloth, or asphalt rubber, or polymeric film. **All such coatings must be completely removed before splicing and concreting**, otherwise bond is greatly reduced.

##### 2.5.1.5 Concrete cover (ระยะหุ้มคอนกรีต) [REQ] (pp. 61–66)

**Definition**: cover = distance from the concrete surface to the **outermost surface of single ties (เหล็กปลอกเดี่ยว), spirals (เหล็กปลอกเกลียว) or stirrups (เหล็กลูกตั้ง)**; where none exist, to the surface of the outermost reinforcement.

Minimum cover for reinforcement **shall** comply with **มยผ. 1332** (DPT standard 1332, "Concrete work standard considering durability and service life"; year not stated).

For durability against deterioration and to prevent reinforcement corrosion, RC structures **shall** have cover **not less than the minimum cover**, computed from Eqs. (2.1) and (2.2):

- **(2.1)  c ≥ c_min**
- **(2.2)  c_min = α · c0**

where
- **c0** = recommended (basic/"general") cover for reinforcement (ระยะหุ้มเหล็กเสริมทั่วไป), from **Table 2.22, Table 2.23 and Table 2.24**;
- **α** = cover coefficient, depending on concrete quality / compressive strength, from **Table 2.21**;
- **c_min** = minimum cover required for durability in every environmental condition;
- **c** = the cover used: the calculated value **rounded up to the next whole multiple of 5 mm** (ปัดเศษขึ้นเป็นจำนวนเต็มครั้งละ 5 มม.).

How it works: pick c0 from the applicable table(s) for the construction type / exposure (general Table 2.22; corrosion-risk Table 2.23; fire-resistance Table 2.24), multiply by α for the concrete's w/b or f'c, then round up to a 5 mm multiple. The standard lists all three tables as sources of c0 but does not spell out a combination rule; logically the largest applicable c0 governs (office reading, Appendix C #6).
Worked examples (office): c0 = 40 mm, f'c = 18 MPa (α = 1.2) → 48 → **c = 50 mm**; c0 = 40 mm, f'c = 45 MPa (α = 0.9) → 36 → **40 mm**; c0 = 20 mm, f'c = 45 MPa → α forced to 1.0 (see note) → **20 mm**.

### 2.6 Storage of materials (การเก็บวัสดุ) (pp. 66–70)

#### 2.6.1 Storage of cement (การเก็บปูนซีเมนต์) [REQ]
1. Cement **should** be stored separated by type, not mixed, in an area protected from moisture.
2. Cement silos **shall** be designed so that no cement remains permanently in the silo.
3. Cement stored for a long time **should** be tested before use.
4. If cement temperature is too high, it **shall** be cooled before use.

Commentary [REC]:
1. Exposed cement reacts with air moisture then CO2 ("weathering"), lowering its specific gravity and the strength of concrete → store in a dry, enclosed place. Airtight silos (no condensation inside) keep cement unchanged for a long time. Silo capacity should normally be **≥ average daily cement consumption**, unless continuous delivery to the silo is possible. Bagged cement in humid locations should be kept in moisture-proof bags; do **not** place bags directly on the floor or against walls — leave a gap of about **30 cm** (condensation on warehouse walls). Do **not stack more than 10 bags** high (compaction of lower bags).
2. Silos should be symmetric, with a steep bottom cone or vibrators / air-blowing system to prevent blockage; inspect and clean regularly.
3. **Lumpy cement should not be used**; long-stored cement should be quality-checked even if no lumps; use first-in-first-out.
4. Cement **8 °C** hotter raises concrete temperature by about **1 °C**. Very hot cement may cause abnormal setting or rapid slump loss. Cement below **50 °C** generally has no adverse effect.

#### 2.6.2 Storage of aggregates (การเก็บมวลรวม) [REQ]
1. Aggregates **should** be stored separated by type, size and grading. Coarse aggregate with maximum size **≥ 60 mm** should be split into **at least 2 size fractions** stored separately.
2. Storage and handling **shall not** cause segregation, contamination, or breakage of aggregate.
3. Storage areas/silos should be good and adequately large, with drainage, to keep the aggregate **surface water** fairly constant for uniform concrete.
4. Aggregates **should** be protected from direct sun (drying and heating → hot concrete); also avoid excessive wetting.

Commentary [REC]:
1. Uniform concrete requires uniform aggregate → keep types/sizes/gradings separate; ≥ 60 mm max-size aggregate split into ≥ 2 sizes to reduce segregation.
2. Do not drop aggregate from height or let it roll down slopes. Storage floor should be **concrete, not natural ground**, with a surrounding bund; no vehicles with muddy wheels; avoid steel-wheeled vehicles (crush aggregate) — use rubber-tyred.
3. Surface moisture control; storage should be large enough to allow testing incoming aggregate (grading, specific gravity, absorption, surface moisture) and adjusting the mix before use. Porous aggregates (lightweight, blast-furnace-slag aggregate) must be kept from drying (dry aggregate absorbs mix water → rapid slump loss, including just after pumping) — provide wetting equipment and drainage of excess water.
4. Sun/heat-exposed aggregate raises concrete temperature (esp. large summer pours) → rapid workability loss or premature set; overly dry aggregate also causes rapid workability loss; overly wet aggregate gives non-uniform water content (water drains to the bottom). Store to avoid both.

#### 2.6.3 Storage of mineral admixtures (การเก็บแร่ผสมเพิ่ม) [REQ]
1. **Should** be stored separated by type in silos or warehouses, protected from moisture.
2. Long-stored mineral admixtures **should** be quality-checked before use.
3. Take care to prevent dust dispersion.

Commentary [REC]:
1. Most are hygroscopic → lumping and deterioration (e.g. fly ash lumps easily; expansive agents deteriorate faster than cement owing to high free CaO). Store separately by type in moisture-proof silos/areas, use FIFO, arrange for easy identification and inspection.
2. Even if well stored, concrete made with long-stored mineral admixtures should be tested before use.
3. Fine, low-specific-gravity powders disperse easily when bags/silo gates are opened; very fine spherical silica fume / fly ash leaks through small gaps at silo gates even when closed, upsetting batching → ensure gates close tightly. In humid weather material may cake on silo gate walls and conveying equipment.

#### 2.6.4 Storage of chemical admixtures (การเก็บสารเคมีผสมเพิ่ม) [REQ]
1. **Should** be stored protected against dust/dirt contamination. Liquid admixtures should be stored to prevent change of quality, freezing/solidifying, or separation; powder admixtures should be protected from moisture absorption and lumping.
2. Properties of admixtures stored for a long time **should** be verified before use.

Commentary [REC]:
1. Contamination alters quality or clogs pump pipes/valves. Evaporation or rain ingress changes concentration. Do not store where it can freeze. When several admixtures are stored together, prevent confusion, wrong selection, or cross-mixing. Long storage may cause separation/decomposition; rust inside steel drums alters quality; evaporation causes surface skinning; frozen admixture may separate, but can be reused after warming to melt and remixing uniformly (normal warming does not boil off the water). Powder admixtures absorb moisture or melt if very hot → store in dry, not-too-hot places; avoid sun, heat sources, or fire.
2. Admixtures stored for a long time — **normally more than 1 year** — or suspected of quality change, **must** be tested by trial concrete mixing before use (small dosages strongly affect concrete).
3. Some liquid admixtures have settling suspended solids → shake/agitate drums or cans, or use a stirrer, before use; consult the manufacturer whether mixing before use is required.

#### 2.6.5 Storage of reinforcement (การเก็บเหล็กเสริม) [REQ] (p.70)
- Reinforcement **should** be stored in a warehouse or covered area, and **supported so it does not rest directly on the ground**.

Commentary [REC]:
- Keep off the ground to prevent rust and contamination; store under cover to prevent corrosion from rain, dew, or sea spray.
- Support on sleepers/dunnage at suitable spacing for easy handling and inspection.
- **Store separately by bar size and by strength grade.**

## 3. Concrete mix proportions (ส่วนผสมคอนกรีต)

### 3.1 General (ทั่วไป)
- [REQ] Mix proportions recommended in this standard apply **only to conventional concrete (คอนกรีตธรรมดา)**. A good mix uses the **minimum water content** that still gives adequate placeability (workability) for the work/structure, and meets other required properties (strength, durability, watertightness).
- [REC] Strength may be compressive, tensile or flexural; compressive strength is normally used for design and construction of general structures.
- [REC] For fly-ash concrete mix design, refer to the EIT book "Mix Design of Fly Ash Concrete" (การออกแบบส่วนผสมของคอนกรีตผสมเถ้าลอย), published by EIT.

### 3.2 Design strength (กำลังที่ออกแบบ) – required average strength
- [REQ] Mix design shall cover the **specified compressive strength (กำลังอัดกำหนด, f'c)** plus site variability from construction quality control. Generally the mix is designed so that **not more than 5 %** of site-sampled test results fall below f'c; therefore the mix **must** be designed for an **average strength higher than f'c**.
- [REC] (1) Strength varies with raw-material quality (cement, stone, sand), production, and site control (placing, vibrating, curing); hence the mix is designed with an average strength exceeding f'c by a **margin (ส่วนเผื่อ)**.
- [REC] (2) Margin = standard deviation **Sn** × a constant (from the normal distribution) depending on the permitted fraction of results below f'c — Table 3.1.

**Table 3.1 – Required average compressive strength for mix design**

| % of samples below f'c | Margin (constant × SD) | Average strength for mix design |
|---|---|---|
| 5.0 | 1.645 × Sn | f'c + 1.645 Sn |
| 2.5 | 1.960 × Sn | f'c + 1.960 Sn |
| 2.0 | 2.054 × Sn | f'c + 2.054 Sn |

- [REC] Choice of defective fraction depends on accepted risk: general structures ≤ **5 %**; structures requiring low probability of failure due to concrete → **2.5 % or 2.0 %**.
- [REC] Standard deviation obtained from compressive tests of concrete of the designed mix using local materials; **not fewer than 30 test results**, computed as:
  - Sn = √[ (x1² + x2² + … + xn² − n·x̄²) / (n − 1) ]  (MPa)
  - x = individual test strength (MPa); x̄ = (x1 + x2 + … + xn)/n (MPa); n = total number of tests.
- [REC] SD depends on the level of production/testing control — Table 3.2. Where **no strength test data** exist, the average strength shall follow the margins of Table 3.3.

**Table 3.2 – Standard deviation vs. level of control (concrete production and testing)**

| Fair (พอใช้) | > 4.1 to ≤ 4.8 |
| Good (ดี) | > 3.4 to ≤ 4.1 |
| Very good (ดีมาก) | > 2.7 to ≤ 3.4 |

### 3.4 Water content (ปริมาณน้ำในส่วนผสม)
- [REQ] Water should not be excessive — only enough for placeability, to prevent excessive **bleeding (การเยิ้ม)**. Suitable water contents for various slumps using crushed stone and natural sand in **saturated-surface-dry (SSD, สภาพอิ่มตัวผิวแห้ง)** condition are in Table 3.4.

**Table 3.4 – Suitable water content (litres per m³ of concrete)**

| Slump (cm) | Max. aggregate size 25 mm | Max. aggregate size 20 mm |
|---|---|---|
| 7.5 | 170 | 180 |
| 10.0 | 180 | 190 |
| 12.5 | 190 | 200 |
| 15.0 | 200 | 210 |

- [REC] Values are approximate; may need adjustment after trial mixes. With water-reducing (**plasticizer**) or high-range water-reducing (**superplasticizer**) admixtures water can be reduced, but trial mixes should be made before use.
- [REC] For **fly-ash concrete**, consider reducing water to prevent bleeding, which weakens the top surface and reduces abrasion resistance — especially for low-strength designs.

### 3.5 Cement content or binder content (ปริมาณปูนซีเมนต์ / วัสดุประสาน)
- [REQ] Cement (binder) content follows from the specified w/c (w/b). **Avoid excessively high cement/binder contents** — they cause high shrinkage and high heat of hydration.
- [REQ] Cement for structures **must be Portland cement**, of the type appropriate to the work as specified in **Chapter 2**.

### 3.6 Maximum size of coarse aggregate (ขนาดโตสุดของมวลรวมหยาบ)
- [REQ] Max. coarse aggregate size should not exceed the least of:
  - **1/5 of the narrowest dimension** of the member;
  - **1/3 of the slab thickness**;
  - **2/3 of the minimum clear spacing of reinforcement**.
- Max. sizes per Table 3.5.

**Table 3.5 – Maximum coarse aggregate size for various concrete work**

| Type of structure | Max. size (mm) |
|---|---|
| General reinforced concrete | 20 or 25 |
| Massive (very thick) reinforced concrete, or plain (unreinforced) concrete | 40 |

- [REC] Relaxation permitted if the engineer judges, or tests show, that the concrete has good placeability and/or adequate compaction methods exist so that no honeycombing/voids form.

### 3.7 Slump (ค่ายุบตัว)
- [REQ] Slump should be sufficient for placing into forms and compacting by vibration without voids. Slump by type of work: Table 3.6.

**Table 3.6 – Slump for conventional concrete**

| Type of work | Slump (cm) |
|---|---|
| Foundations (ฐานราก) | 5.0 – 7.5 |
| Road pavements, slabs, beams, RC walls | 5.0 – 10.0 |
| General structures | 5.0 – 10.0 |
| Columns | 5.0 – 12.5 |
| Thin walls and RC fins/ribs (ครีบ) | 5.0 – 15.0 |
| Heavily (congested) reinforced structures | 10.0 – 15.0 |

- [REC] Slump depends on water content, aggregate grading and shape, and **sand-to-total-aggregate ratio (s/a, by volume)**. General concrete uses **s/a = 0.35 – 0.44**. Too low s/a → harsh, hard-to-work mix; higher s/a → more water (same slump) → more cement → uneconomical. Table 3.6 is guidance for conventional concrete only; excludes special concretes (self-compacting concrete, roller-compacted concrete, etc.).

### 3.8 Fines content (ปริมาณส่วนละเอียด)
- [REQ] Fines (cement/binder + sand) affect placeability and segregation. For crushed stone and natural sand in Thailand, suitable fines content (absolute volume of cement + sand per m³) per Table 3.7.

**Table 3.7 – Fines content per 1 m³ of concrete**

| Max. stone size (mm) | Fines content |
|---|---|
| 25 | 380 litres (38 %) |
| 20 | 400 litres (40 %) |

- [REC] Absolute-volume mix calculation uses tested specific gravities; if tests are not possible, approximate values (Thai materials): **cement 3.15; sand (SSD) 2.65; crushed limestone (SSD) 2.70**.

### 3.9 Worked example of mix calculation (ตัวอย่างการคำนวณส่วนผสมคอนกรีต) – summary
Designed for 1 m³ of concrete.
1. Data: f'c (cylinder) = **30 MPa**; slump **12.0 cm**; max. stone size **20 mm**.
2. Required average strength: defective fraction not specified → use general value **5 %**; control "Good", SD = **4.2 MPa** → f'cr = 30 + 1.645 × 4.2 = **37 MPa**.
3. w/c from Fig. 3.1 at 37 MPa → **0.53** (see inconsistency note under Fig. 3.1).
4. Water for 12.0 cm slump, Table 3.4 → **200 L/m³**.
5. Cement = 200 / 0.53 ≈ **380 kg/m³**.
6. Sand: Table 3.7, cement + sand volume = 400 L; cement volume = 380/3.15 = 121 L; sand volume = 279 L; sand (SSD) = 279 × 2.65 = **740 kg/m³**.
7. Stone: entrapped air for non-air-entrained concrete ≈ **1 %** = 10 L; stone volume = 1000 − 200 − 121 − 279 − 10 = 390 L; stone (SSD) = 390 × 2.7 = **1060 kg/m³**.
   Result per m³: cement 380 kg; water 200 L; sand 740 kg; stone (20 mm max) 1060 kg; s/a (by volume) = 279/(279 + 390) = **0.42**.
- [REC] After calculation and trial mixing, a good mix must not segregate; check via the slump cone test then lightly tapping the base plate. Inappropriate proportions of cement/stone/sand/water/admixture give harsh, poorly cohesive or segregating mixes (Figs. 3.3–3.7).
- [REC] Liquid admixture is counted as part of the mixing water (e.g. 10 L admixture → water 200 − 10 = 190 L).
- [REC] If sand is wetter than SSD, measure **surface water** and deduct it from added water; when weighing moist sand, allow for surface water so that the SSD sand mass stays constant.
- [REC] To improve cohesion of a fluid mix: increase sand and decrease stone by equal amounts, **40 kg per m³ at a time**, re-trial and repeat until proportions are suitable.
- Figures (photos, no numbers): 3.3 properly proportioned fresh concrete; 3.4 too much sand; 3.5 too little sand; 3.6 too little binder → segregation of aggregate due to lack of paste bond; 3.7 excessive water and/or water-reducer → segregation.

## 4. Batching and mixing of concrete (การชั่งตวงวัสดุผสมและการผสมคอนกรีต)

### 4.1 Batching (การชั่งตวงวัสดุผสม)

#### 4.1.1 General
- [REQ] Each constituent **shall** be measured accurately before mixing to obtain concrete with the required properties.
- [REC] Batching errors strongly affect quality; batch with sufficient accuracy, appropriate to the importance of the work.

#### 4.1.2 Batching equipment
- [REQ] (1) Methods and equipment **shall** suit the work and be capable of measuring correct quantities with sufficient accuracy.
- [REQ] (2) Batching equipment **shall** be checked and calibrated regularly **before use**, and re-checked/re-calibrated periodically **during use**.
- [REC] (1) Choose equipment whose capacity and accuracy are acceptable for the work. (2) Equipment needs regular periodic checks throughout its service life.

#### 4.1.3 Batching of materials
- [REQ] (1) Materials **shall** be batched in their as-used condition. If moist, test **surface moisture** of aggregates by standard or other suitable method; if dry, test **effective absorption**; use results to correct batch quantities.
- [REQ] (2) Batch size depends on type of construction, quantity of concrete, mixer, transport method and site conditions.
- [REQ] (3) Materials should be batched **by mass (weight)**, except **water and admixtures**, which may be batched **by volume**.
- [REQ] (4) Batching errors should not exceed Table 4.1.
- [REQ] (5) For **continuous mixers**, materials may be batched by volume; the error when converted to mass should not exceed Table 4.1.

**Table 4.1 – Permissible batching tolerances (ค่าความคลาดเคลื่อนที่ยอมให้)**

| Material | Permissible tolerance |
|---|---|
| Water | 1 % for all batch quantities |
| Cement | 2 % when batch < 200 kg; 1 % when batch ≥ 200 kg |
| All aggregates | 3 % when batch < 500 kg; 2 % when batch ≥ 500 kg |
| Mineral admixtures (แร่ผสมเพิ่ม) | 3 % for all batch quantities |
| Chemical admixtures (สารเคมีผสมเพิ่ม) | 3 % for all batch quantities |

- [REC] (1) Aggregate surface moisture and effective absorption strongly affect mixing water; batch to match field condition of materials (moisture changes with time and storage). Choose a surface-moisture test method suited to the job (time, accuracy, economy). Effective absorption: theoretically **24 h** soaking; in practice **15 to 30 min** is considered adequate. Water used to dilute admixtures and the admixture itself count as mixing water.
- [REC] (2) Batch size strongly affects efficiency and cost; consider type of work, quantity, plant capacity and transport.
- [REC] (3) Normally batch by mass; water and liquid admixtures may be batched by volume.
- [REC] (4) Two error sources: weighing equipment (correct by regular checking — scale accuracy of about **0.5 % of maximum capacity** is sufficient for concrete work) and material delivery to the scale (check delivery devices regularly). Keep equipment clean (dust affects accuracy). Table 4.1 is for general construction; for very important work tighter tolerances are usually specified: **1 % for cement and water, 2 % for aggregates and admixtures**. Take great care when the batched quantity is small relative to scale capacity.
- [REC] (5) Continuous mixers (mass or volume per unit time): check batching devices regularly using the actual job materials.

### 4.2 Mixing (การผสมคอนกรีต)

#### 4.2.1 General
- [REQ] All materials **shall** be mixed until homogeneous.
- [REC] Use a high-efficiency mixer and appropriate charging sequence.

#### 4.2.2 Mixers
- [REQ] (1) Any type of mixer **shall** always be checked for conformance with the standard specification.
- [REQ] (2) No mixer **shall** cause segregation of fresh concrete on discharge.
- [REC] Check mixer performance by actual mixing and sampling: if variation of **mortar mass per unit volume > 0.8 %** or variation of **coarse-aggregate mass per unit volume > 5 %**, the mixer is usually deemed unsuitable or excessively worn and must be repaired. Also test compressive strength or other required properties to confirm mixer efficiency.

#### 4.2.3 Mixing
- [REQ] (1) Charging sequence and mixing time **shall** be fixed in advance, based mainly on trial-mix results.
- [REQ] (2) Interior of the mixer should be **buttered (coated) with mortar** before mixing concrete.
- [REQ] (3) Do not charge the next batch until the previous batch has been completely discharged.
- [REQ] (4) Mixer **shall** be cleaned before and after every use.
- [REQ] (5) Concrete left until it has begun to set **shall not be reused** (no retempering of set concrete).
- [REC] (1) Proper sequence depends on mixer type, mixing time, aggregate grading/type, water, cement/binder content and admixture type; in practice set by supervisor experience or strength/bleeding test results. Site-added admixtures shall be added per the standard method. **Do not charge cement and water first** (without aggregate the mixer cannot mix paste efficiently; paste sticks to the drum wall).
- [REC] (2) **Mixing time**: depends on mixer type and capacity, proportions, admixtures and charging order — determine by trial mixing. Mixing is generally considered complete when variation of mortar mass per unit volume **< 0.8 %** and of coarse aggregate mass per unit volume **< 5 %**. Mixing time should **not exceed 3 × the specified mixing time**. Over-mixing (especially large mixers, large or weak coarse aggregate) reduces workability and increases slump loss (aggregate breakdown → more fines/dust) and reduces entrained air; if long mixing is unavoidable, mixing may be stopped intermittently.
- [REC] (3) The first batch usually does not meet design properties because mortar sticks to the drum; mix a mortar batch before the actual concrete.
- [REC] (4) When material properties or produced-concrete properties change, wash the mixer clean and proceed as in (3).
- (See also 5.4.3 [REC] for TIS 213 minimum mixing times.)

## 5. Ready-mixed concrete (คอนกรีตผสมเสร็จ)

### 5.1 General
- [REQ] 5.1.1 Requirements not covered here: refer to **มอก. 213** (TIS 213, ready-mixed concrete; edition cited in commentary: **มอก. 213-2520**).
- [REQ] 5.1.2 Trading units: volume in **m³**, slump in **cm**, compressive strength in **MPa**.
- [REQ] 5.1.3 The **producer** is responsible for checking raw materials (cement, stone, sand, water, admixtures) for conformance with standards, and for production and delivery up to the purchaser's site, so the concrete meets the purchaser's requirements.
- [REQ] 5.1.4 The **purchaser** calculates the volume of fresh concrete required for each pour and notifies the producer to produce and deliver.
- [REC] (1) TIS 213 does not cover placing and curing. (2) Purchaser may request the producer's **mix proportion submittal** to confirm properties and full yield per m³. (3) Delivered volume may be insufficient due to over-excavation, form deflection/spreading, loss of air, or shrinkage on hardening — check these carefully.

### 5.2 Raw materials (วัตถุดิบที่ใช้ในการผลิต)
Materials shall conform to:
- [REQ] 5.2.1 Cement: properties per **clause 2.1**, storage per **clause 2.6**.
- [REQ] 5.2.2 Aggregates: properties per **clause 2.3**, storage per **clause 2.6**.
- [REQ] 5.2.3 Mixing water **shall** be stored in containers preventing loss and contamination; properties per **clause 2.2**.
- [REQ] 5.2.4 Admixtures (น้ำยาผสมคอนกรีต): properties per **clause 2.4**.

### 5.3 Selection of ready-mixed concrete plant (การเลือกใช้โรงงานคอนกรีตผสมเสร็จ)
- [REQ] Consider delivery time, placing time, plant production capacity, number of trucks required, raw-material quality control, production process control and testing.
- [REC] (1) Choose a plant near the site to minimise haul time. Normally, **with suitable admixture**, haul time **plus placing time should not exceed 2 hours**; **without admixture, not more than 1 hour**.
- [REC] (2) Plant capacity should match the continuous placing rate (possibly > 1 plant); sufficient trucks for the placing rate.
- [REC] (3) Check concrete properties suit requirements (strength, workability, setting time).
- [REC] (4) An experienced responsible person should be stationed at the plant to resolve problems.
- [REC] (5) Plant should have raw-material receiving inspection and quality checking systems.
- [REC] (6) Plant should have good production control and QC per **clause 5.6**.

### 5.4 Ready-mixed concrete plant (โรงงานคอนกรีตผสมเสร็จ)
- [REQ] Stockpile raw materials separately and clearly without intermixing; discharge into weigh hoppers **must** not cause segregation; weighing **must** be accurate; measuring instruments must be clearly visible to the operator to avoid batching errors.

#### 5.4.1 Types of plant (two types by mixing method)
- [REQ] (1) **Central-mixed concrete (การผสมกับที่)**: mixed in a stationary plant mixer (drum-type, pan-type, or trough mixer – single- or twin-shaft), delivered by **mixer truck** or **agitator truck** (per 5.5) to the point of placement. The mixer should have a **batch mixing timer**, timing starting when the **first material enters the mixer**.
  - [REC] (1) Inspect/clean the plant mixer **daily** so hardened concrete does not build up on blades. (2) Truck drums should rotate at the manufacturer's specified (agitating) speed while delivering.
- [REQ] (2) **Truck-mixed concrete (การผสมโดยรถ)**: materials batched at the plant into a **mixer truck** (per 5.5.1) and mixed in the truck until uniform per **clause 4.2**.

#### 5.4.2 Checking and calibration of weighing/measuring equipment
- [REQ] Weighing/measuring equipment in the plant should be checked **daily**, with a calibration plan **monthly** or according to production volume.
- [REC] TIS 213-2520 tolerances:
  - (1) Cement: by mass on a **dedicated cement scale**; ±1 % if batch ≥ 200 kg; ±2 % if < 200 kg.
  - (2) Aggregates: by mass; ±2 % if batch ≥ 500 kg; ±3 % if < 500 kg.
  - (3) Water: by mass or volume, total including wash water retained in drum, aggregate moisture and added water: ±3 %. (For tolerance on mixing-water batching follow **clause 4.1.3**, i.e. Table 4.1 = 1 %.)
  - (4) Admixtures: powder by mass; liquid by mass or volume; ±3 %.

#### 5.4.3 Mixing time
- [REQ] Mixing time for each mix determined by **uniformity testing** per **clause 4.2**.
- [REC] TIS 213-2520 mixing times:
  - (1) Central mixing, without uniformity test: **≥ 1 min for ≤ 0.75 m³**, plus **≥ 15 s for each additional 0.75 m³**.
  - (2) Truck mixing, without uniformity test: **70 to 100 revolutions** of the drum at the manufacturer's rated **mixing speed**.

### 5.5 Truck mixers / delivery trucks (รถขนส่งคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Mixer drums **must** be made of good material preventing ingress of external water into the concrete.
- [REQ] 5.5.1 **Mixer truck (รถโม่ผสมคอนกรีต)**: drum with mixing blades (blades at drum rear, or double spiral blades throughout the drum); suitable for both truck-mixed and central-mixed plants.
  - [REC] (1) From truck-mixed plants: load **≤ 65 %** and **≥ 50 %** of drum volume for best mixing efficiency. (2) From central-mixed plants: load **≤ 80 %** of drum volume.
- [REQ] 5.5.2 **Agitator truck (รถกวน)**: drum with spiral blades only for charging and discharging; suitable **only** for central-mixed plants.
  - [REC] Load **≤ 80 %** of total volume, especially on steep grades, to avoid spillage.
- [REQ] 5.5.3 Other trucks, e.g. **dump truck (รถบรรทุกเทท้าย)**: suitable for **low-slump** concrete, short hauls; concrete **must be fully discharged within 30 min after start of mixing**.
  - [REC] Slump for dump-truck haul should **not exceed 5 cm** to avoid segregation in transit.

### 5.6 Specification of concrete quality (การกำหนดคุณภาพของคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Producer **must** satisfy the purchaser that standard-conforming materials and reliable production control and testing are used.

#### 5.6.1 Mix specification
- [REQ] (1) Purchaser may prescribe the mix proportions for the producer to follow. (2) Producer is responsible for designing the mix to achieve the purchaser's required characteristics (performance specification).

#### 5.6.2 Material specification
- [REQ] Purchaser may specify materials, e.g. cement type, **minimum cement/binder content** per mix, maximum aggregate size, admixture type, for the producer's mix design.

#### 5.6.3 Slump
- [REQ] Where the purchaser's specification gives no slump tolerance, the producer **must** keep slump within Table 5.1.

**Table 5.1 – Slump tolerance**

| Specified slump (cm) | Tolerance (cm) |
|---|---|
| 5 – 15 | ± 2.5 |
| > 15 | ± 3.5 |

- [REC] (1) Place concrete **within 30 min after the slump test at the point of placement**; beyond 30 min, placing may continue if slump is still adequate for placing and compaction.
- [REC] (2) Site slump adjustment **must not be by adding water**; adjust by adding **superplasticizer** (type and dosage per admixture manufacturer), then rotate drum **≈ 30 revolutions or more** at mixing speed until slump is uniform throughout the drum. The superplasticizer must not adversely affect setting time or compressive strength.
- [REC] (3) Perform slump tests when strength specimens are taken, and random tests to confirm consistency, at the discretion of the supervising engineer.

#### 5.6.4 Compressive strength
- [REQ] (1) Where strength is specified, compression testing per **มอก. 409** (TIS 409).
- [REQ] (2) Sampling frequency: at least **1 sample (set) per day** and **1 set per pour**; for pours **> 50 m³**, **1 set per 50 m³**; for **slabs and walls**, **1 set per 250 m²** of placement and at least once per day — subject to the supervising engineer's judgement.
- [REQ] (3) Each set **at least 3 specimens**; **average** of results must not be below the specified strength, and **each individual specimen** must not be below **85 % of specified strength**.
- [REC] (1) Where early-age results (< 7 days) are needed, e.g. prestressed concrete needing 1–3-day strengths, purchaser should notify producer in advance for extra specimens.
- [REC] (2) If tests fail (possibly due to production or testing), in-place testing may be done: **core test per ASTM C42 or ACI 318**; **load test per ACI 318** (strength evaluation of existing structures); or **ultrasonic pulse velocity per ASTM C597**.
- [REC] (3) For mass concrete, sampling follows the mass-concrete QC specification; prestressed concrete sampling may have special procedures not covered here.

#### 5.6.5 Delivery information (delivery ticket)
- [REQ] Delivery documents should contain details per **มอก. 213**.
- [REC] TIS 213-2520 requires: (1) plant/producer or distributor name or registered trademark; (2) ticket serial number; (3) truck number; (4) purchaser name; (5) job name and location; (6) type and grade (class) of concrete; (7) quantity of concrete; (8) **time mixing started and time discharge completed**; (9) slump; (10) maximum aggregate size; (11) admixtures.

### 5.7 Coordination of concrete delivery (การประสานงานการส่งมอบคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] 5.7.1 Delivery schedule, concrete type and quantity, placement locations, delivery rate etc. should be planned and agreed with the producer in advance, with appointments set.
- [REQ] 5.7.2 Continuous coordination between site and plant during the pour.
- [REQ] 5.7.3 Site access and discharge points should be convenient and safe for rapid placing.
- [REQ] 5.7.4 Placing should minimise segregation.
- [REQ] 5.7.5 Quality inspection of concrete per **clause 5.6**.
- [REC] (1) Coordinate in advance so producer can choose plant, capacity, number of trucks, materials; site prepares labour, placing equipment, access, and a safe/convenient specimen-sampling location. (2) Check trucks/placing equipment are suitable to minimise segregation. (3) Prepare slump testing, specimen sampling and other tests in advance.

## 6. Conveying concrete (การลำเลียงคอนกรีต)

### 6.1 General
- [REQ] Conveying of mixed concrete **must** keep it uniform and prevent segregation before placing in forms; concrete **must** be protected from environment/weather (temperature, heat, humidity).
- [REQ] Selection of conveying method depends on: (1) quantity and rate of placing per pour; (2) size and type of structure; (3) terrain, site and haul route; (4) costs (labour, plant/equipment).
- [REC] Minimise conveying time using the most suitable, economical method so properties stay unchanged and uniform; plan every pour considering:
  - (1) Concrete selection – besides strength, suit the concrete to placing in the forms; choose conveying method considering setting time and consistency (setting time depends on mix, materials, admixtures, temperature, humidity, conveying method).
  - (2) Placing plan – consider concrete properties, structure type, placing method, quantity per pour, ease of placing, weather, etc.
  - (3) Equipment and labour for conveying and placing – conveying should be fast and economical to reduce segregation and changes in uniformity/workability; decide number/types of equipment and workers.
  - (4) Conveying route – prepare before placing for speed and minimum time.
  - (5) Inspection – during conveying, check that concrete remains uniform and unsegregated.

### 6.2 Conveying methods (วิธีการลำเลียงคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Choose method according to mixing location vs. placement area, avoiding segregation:
  - (1) Mixing point **at same level** as placement: labour, wheelbarrows/carts, truck mixer, belt conveyor, or concrete pump.
  - (2) Mixing point **higher** than placement: chutes (ราง), belt conveyor, or concrete pump.
  - (3) Mixing point **lower** than placement: hoists/pulleys, lifts, mobile crane, tower crane, belt conveyor, or concrete pump.
  - (4) Mixing point **far** from placement: **must** use truck mixers to site, then convey onward by another suitable method.

### 6.3 Conveying by vehicle (การลำเลียงโดยรถ)
- [REQ] 6.3.1 **Wheelbarrows / small buggies (รถเข็น / รถกระบะ)**: for mixer close to placement point and small pour quantities.
  - [REC] Beware of segregation, especially with very fluid concrete.
- [REQ] 6.3.2 **Truck mixer (รถโม่)**: for mixing point far from placement, or large quantities.
  - [REC] Suitable for high-slump (fluid) concrete as it prevents segregation in transit. Two types: (1) **Mixer truck** – mixes thoroughly; for dry-mix (truck-mixed) plants; drum with rear blades or double spiral blades throughout. (2) **Agitator truck** – transport only; for wet-mix (central-mixed) plants; spiral blades only for charging/discharging.
- [REQ] 6.3.3 **Dump truck (รถกระบะเทท้าย)**: for airfields, roads, large slabs/yards using low-consistency concrete, e.g. **slump < 5 cm**; requires compaction equipment such as a **concrete paver**; mixing point should not be far from placement.
  - [REC] Beware of coarse-aggregate segregation on discharge — dumping directly onto the ground may scatter coarse aggregate.

### 6.4 Conveying by buckets (การลำเลียงโดยกระบะ, Buckets)
- [REQ] Suitable for placing rates of about **20 m³/h**; one of the good methods to prevent segregation.
- [REC] Crane-handled buckets suit medium-to-large projects (reach almost any point vertically and horizontally). Do **not leave concrete in the bucket for long** — loss of fluidity and possible segregation.

### 6.5 Conveying by concrete pump (การลำเลียงโดยปั๊มคอนกรีต)
- [REQ] Suitable for continuous large-volume placing; rates **> 40 m³/h** possible depending on pump type/size. Two types:
  - (1) **Mobile pump** – truck-mounted, easily moved but limited by height (boom reach).
  - (2) **Stationary pump** – towed to location, with **steel pipeline Ø 10–15 cm** run up the building; higher output and pressure than mobile, but relocating and pipe connection are more cumbersome.
- [REC] (1) Consider the mix and materials for pumpability; select pump type/size for structure, placing rate and lift height.
- [REC] (2) Low-height pours at many points simultaneously → mobile pump; high-level pours → stationary pump, with **flexible hose at the end of the steel pipeline** for mobility.
- [REC] (3) Pipe diameter selection considers pipe weight; pipe anchorage to the building must be rigid to prevent vibration during pumping. **Max. coarse aggregate size ≤ 1/5 of pipe diameter**. Coat pipe interior with **mortar** (or other lubricant) **before every use**.
- [REC] (4) Minimise bends; avoid **90° elbows** (high pumping resistance, reduced efficiency).

### 6.6 Conveying by belt conveyor (การลำเลียงโดยสายพานลำเลียง, Belt conveyor)
- [REQ] Suitable for **low-slump (low-fluidity)** concrete and continuous placing; usable upward, downward or level. Segregation is small as all material moves together, but guard against **water loss from wind and sun**.
- [REC] Do not use for fluid concrete on **inclined** routes (segregation). Watch conveying distance — long distances cause water loss from wind and sun.

## 7. Placing and consolidation (การเทคอนกรีต และการทำให้แน่น) — pp.103–108

### 7.1 Preparation for placing and placing (การเตรียมเทคอนกรีตและการเทคอนกรีต)

#### 7.1.1 Preparation before placing (การเตรียมเทคอนกรีต)
- **7.1.1.1 [REQ]** Reinforcement quantity and position shall be checked against the design; formwork and other items shall be checked against the plan; reinforcement surface shall be clean — free of soil, mud, oil or other deleterious material that would impair bond between steel and concrete.
- **7.1.1.2 [REQ]** Inspect conveying equipment, placing equipment and inner faces of formwork so no debris (soil, mud, wood chips, etc.) contaminates the concrete. Inner faces of equipment and forms should be pre-moistened to prevent absorption of water from the concrete.
- **7.1.1.3 [REQ]** When placing in pits/excavations (หลุมหรือบ่อ), remaining water should be removed before placing, and water should be prevented from flowing into the pit during placing and while the concrete is freshly placed.
- **[REC]**
  - Tied reinforcement shall be secured firmly enough not to displace during placing; forms likewise sufficiently strong. Debris lowers strength locally. Absorption of mix water by forms/equipment gives rough formed surfaces; pre-wet forms but do not leave standing water in them.
  - For long-distance pumping through pipes, first pump a mortar of the same composition as the mortar fraction of the concrete (lubricating the pipe) to avoid loss of mortar coating the pipe at start of pumping.
  - When placing onto old/hardened or partially set concrete, first place a mortar of the same composition as the concrete's mortar to improve bond.
  - Water left in a pit changes the mix (lower strength and durability) — remove it. Water flowing over fresh/unhardened concrete washes out mortar, spoiling appearance and reducing local strength/durability.

#### 7.1.2 Placing (การเทคอนกรีต) [REQ]
- Placing should be planned so it proceeds continuously and efficiently without obstructing unrelated work.
- Good placing gives uniform concrete with no segregation (การแยกตัว) and no honeycomb. Concrete should not be dropped directly against reinforcement or form sides; it should be deposited vertically; it should not be made to flow horizontally over long distances — except self-compacting/flowing concrete (คอนกรีตไหล) designed with controlled segregation.
- If segregation is observed after placing starts, it shall be corrected immediately.
- When placing in successive layers, each upper layer should be placed before the lower layer begins to set (initial set).
- **Free-fall height:** In tall forms, concrete should not free-fall from the top of the form; use belt conveyor, chute, bucket or extended pipe (tremie/elephant trunk) so that **free-fall height ≤ 1.5 m**.
- If bleeding (การเยิ้ม) is observed during placing, stop placing until bleed water on the surface is removed before placing the next layer.
- **Rate of placing** for tall members (columns, walls) placed continuously should not be too fast — normally about **2–3 m/h (vertical rise)**.
- **[REC]**
  - Segregation causes honeycomb (Honey-comb) because separated coarse aggregate clogs congested reinforcement.
  - Placing can displace reinforcement/forms; steel fixers and form carpenters should stand by during placing to correct displacement immediately.
  - Do not use vibrators to move concrete laterally; vibrators are only for compaction.
  - Segregation observed in placed concrete indicates an unsuitable mix — correct the mix immediately before continuing.
  - Place as continuously as possible to avoid cold joints (lower bond at discontinuities).
  - Excessive drop height leaves mortar hanging on upper bars/forms that hardens and obstructs later placing, gives poor surfaces, and causes segregation from aggregate striking bars/forms — hence the free-fall limit.
  - In tall members (columns/walls) water migrates upward under self-weight causing bleeding that accumulates under bars and under coarse aggregate, reducing steel–concrete bond and paste–aggregate bond.
  - In walls, place concrete moving away from corners rather than toward them.
  - When placing in layers, level the top of each layer horizontally (or nearly so) before placing the next.

### 7.2 Consolidation (การทำให้แน่น)

#### 7.2.1 General [REQ]
- During placing, concrete shall be thoroughly compacted by hand tools, vibrators or finishing machines, to obtain dense concrete with good bond to reinforcement and a smooth surface.
- Particular compaction around reinforcement, embedded items and at form corners. External tapping of side forms with a hammer may help fill corners, but should not be overdone — overworking causes segregation: water and fines rise, collect under bars and large aggregate, reducing bond and forming channels through which water can flow.

#### 7.2.2 Hand rodding/tamping (การกระทุ้งด้วยมือ) [REQ]
- For placeable (plastic) concrete, rod through the full depth of the layer being placed and penetrate into the underlying layer by about **10 cm**. Spading with a trowel/spade against or near vertical forms reduces surface roughness and air voids.
- For relatively dry concrete, tamp with a heavy flat-faced tool until a thin film of mortar/paste appears at the surface (indicating aggregate voids are filled).
- **[REC]** Hand rodding suits small pours or very fluid concrete. Rod may be plain round or deformed bar, **diameter ≥ 16 mm**.

#### 7.2.3 Mechanical vibration (การเขย่าด้วยเครื่อง) [REQ]
- Mechanical vibration allows low-slump concrete to be compacted in deep, narrow forms and congested reinforcement where hand compaction is inadequate. Fluid, high-slump concrete must be hand-compacted (rodded). Coarse-aggregate segregation caused by improper placing cannot be corrected by vibration.
- Three types of vibrators: internal (immersion/poker) vibrators (เครื่องเขย่าภายใน), surface vibrators (วางบนผิวคอนกรีต), and form (external) vibrators (ตรึงติดกับแบบหล่อ).
- **[REC]**
  - Insertion spacing short enough that vibrated zones overlap with no gaps; continue until concrete is fully consolidated and voids filled — surface appears smooth/glistening with aggregate embedded. Over-vibration causes coarse aggregate to sink and water/paste to rise.
  - **Normal vibration time 5–15 seconds per insertion at points spaced 45–75 cm apart.**
  - Internal (poker) vibrator: insert vertically to full depth of the layer; do not drag horizontally; insert and withdraw slowly with the vibrator running during withdrawal so no hole remains; do not use to move concrete (causes segregation, stone left around long-held insertions).
  - Surface vibrator: must compact the layer through its full thickness; if not possible, reduce layer thickness or use a more powerful vibrator.
  - Form vibrator: suitable only for thin sections or locations internal vibrators cannot reach.
  - Care in selection, fixing and frequent relocation of vibrators. Thorough vibration of shallow layers greatly reduces air bubbles (e.g., precast pipes). Vibrating into the lower layer or vibrating reinforcement causes only minor damage to partly set concrete, but damage beyond what re-vibration can repair must never occur; re-vibration is beneficial only while concrete has not begun to set.
  - Re-vibration (การเขย่าซ้ำ) is very beneficial as long as the poker still sinks into the concrete under its own weight; it eliminates horizontal cracking and reduces settlement-shrinkage cracks from concrete hung up on reinforcement or rough forms.

#### 7.2.4 Eliminating plastic-shrinkage and settlement cracks [REQ]
- If plastic shrinkage cracks (รอยร้าวเนื่องจากการหดตัวแบบพลาสติก) or settlement cracks (การทรุดตัว) form before the concrete hardens, the concrete should be recompacted immediately by troweling/tamping the surface to close the cracks.
- **[REC]**
  - Where slabs/beams are monolithic with walls or columns, place the slab/beam concrete after settlement of the wall/column concrete has ended (to avoid settlement cracking); same for structures with cantilevers/overhangs.
  - Settlement magnitude depends on section area; with one continuous pour cracks form at changes of section. Pause placing at changes in cross-section; place the upper part (incl. overhangs) after settlement of the lower part is complete. Settlement period depends on mix, materials, temperature — **normally 1–2 hours**.

## 8. Curing (การบ่มคอนกรีต) — pp.109–112

### 8.1 General [REQ]
- Concrete shall be cured immediately after placing is completed and should continue to be cured until it reaches the required strength. Good curing: prevents moisture loss (from heat or wind), prevents excessive heating or cooling, prevents contact with harmful chemicals, and prevents wash-out by rain of freshly placed concrete.

### 8.2 Wet (moist) curing (การบ่มเปียก) [REQ]
- In general, concrete shall be protected from moisture loss due to sun and wind from completion of placing until it begins to harden; after that, exposed surfaces shall be kept continuously wet, e.g., by covering with wet burlap sacks, wet cloth, or by water spraying (ฉีดน้ำให้ชุ่ม).
- **Minimum continuous wet-curing durations:**

| Cement / binder | Minimum wet curing |
|---|---|
| Portland cement Type 1 (ปูนซีเมนต์ปอร์ตแลนด์ประเภทที่ 1) | ≥ 7 days continuous |
| Portland cement Type 3 (high-early-strength) | ≥ 3 days |
| Concrete containing pozzolan (วัสดุปอซโซลาน) | > 7 days, depending on type and amount of pozzolan |

- **[REC]**
  - Inadequately cured concrete does not develop strength (hydration needs water); surface moisture loss causes cracking.
  - Burlap/cloth should be thick enough not to dry quickly and must be kept saturated throughout curing.
  - Concrete with pozzolan partially replacing cement should be cured longer than plain concrete (pozzolanic reaction follows hydration); the higher the replacement, the longer the curing. For low pozzolan content, e.g., **≤ 10–15 % of total binder**, curing may be as for ordinary concrete.
  - Table 8.1 (below) gives curing levels for fly-ash concrete.

**Table 8.1 — Recommended curing level for fly-ash concrete (ตารางที่ 8.1 ข้อแนะนำสำหรับระดับการบ่มคอนกรีตผสมเถ้าลอย)** (p.110; fly-ash classes 1, 2a, 2b, 3 as classified elsewhere in the standard — not defined on this page)

| Replacement (%) | Class 1 (ประเภทที่ 1) | Class 2a (ประเภทที่ 2 ก) | Class 2b (ประเภทที่ 2 ข, high CaO) | Class 3 (ประเภทที่ 3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 – 20 | + | + + | + | + + + |
| 20 – 30 | + + | + + | + + | + + + |
| 30 – 40 | + + | + + + | + + + | + + + |
| > 40 | + + + | + + + | + + + | + + + |

Notes:
- `+` = cure the same as ordinary concrete without fly ash.
- `+ +` = cure longer than ordinary concrete without fly ash.
- `+ + +` = cure much longer than ordinary concrete without fly ash; curing period at this level **may not exceed 21 days** (i.e., up to 21 days).
- [REC] Some special concretes (e.g., rapid-hardening concrete) need not be cured as long, provided the mix was designed to achieve required properties with the effect of short curing already accounted for.

### 8.3 Controlled-temperature curing (การบ่มแบบควบคุมอุณหภูมิ) [REQ]
- Required for some work, especially mass concrete (คอนกรีตหลา).
- In very low ambient temperatures: may require control of fresh-concrete temperature or curing by wrapping with thermal insulation.
- In very high ambient temperatures or mass concrete (risk of large internal–external temperature difference), reduce initial temperature by: cooling cement/binders; using cool sand and stone; chilled mixing water; using materials reducing heat of hydration (e.g., fly ash partial cement replacement); embedded cooling-water pipes circulating cold water; insulating the concrete surface to reduce internal temperature differentials; or a combination.
- **[REC]** Very high/low temperatures cause internal temperature differentials → internal stresses → cracking; very low temperatures can freeze mix water and crack the concrete; hence controlled-temperature curing is needed. (No numeric temperature limits given.)

### 8.4 Accelerated curing (การบ่มแบบเร่งกำลัง) [REQ]
- Used e.g. in precast production: steam curing or high-pressure steam (autoclave) curing.
- Factors to control: delay period before curing starts; rate of temperature rise; maximum curing temperature; duration at maximum temperature; rate of cooling. These shall be established from tests or experience to avoid harming the concrete.
- Accelerated curing shall give strength not less than the design strength and shall have no adverse effect on durability. (No numeric values given.)

### 8.5 Curing compounds (สารเคมีสำหรับการบ่ม) [REQ]
- Normally used only when wet curing is not possible.
- Spray onto the surface, **applied in more than one pass (> 1 coat)** to give sufficient membrane thickness; spray as soon as the surface begins to dry (so surface water does not mix with the compound). If spraying cannot be done at that moment, keep the surface wet by water spraying until it can be applied.
- **[REC]** Do not spray compound on reinforcement or construction joints (bond needed for later concrete). Do not choose curing compound if normal wet curing is possible; use only when necessary and **only with approval of the responsible engineer**.

### 8.6 Curing precautions (ข้อควรระวังสำหรับการบ่ม) [REQ]
- Avoid during curing, especially at early age: vibration, impact, overloading, and large temperature changes within a short time.
- For high-temperature accelerated curing, control the curing temperature so it is not too high — risk of **Delayed Ettringite Formation (DEF)** in the long term. (No numeric max temperature given.)

### 8.7 Acceptance criteria for curing method (เกณฑ์การยอมรับวิธีการบ่ม) [REQ]
- Acceptance of curing method shall be per standards such as **ACI 318**.

## 9. Joints (รอยต่อ, Joints) — pp.113–120

### 9 (general) [REQ]
- Joint locations and details should be specified in the design. If not shown on drawings, the location, direction and construction method shall be determined so that joints do not impair strength, durability or appearance.

### 9.1 Construction joints (รอยต่อก่อสร้าง, Construction joints) [REQ]
1. As a general rule, construction joints should be located where **shear is minimum** and oriented **perpendicular to the direction of compressive force** in the member.
2. If a construction joint must be at a high-shear location, it should be strengthened by a key (เดือย), shear notch/sawtooth (ปากฉลาม), or additional reinforcement.
3. Joint detailing should consider cracking from temperature and drying shrinkage (การหดตัวแบบแห้ง, Drying shrinkage).
- **[REC]**
  1. Shear capacity at joints is uncertain → place at low shear; making the joint normal to compression increases shear transfer across it.
  2. At high-shear joints use keys, sawtooth, added dowel bars across the joint or other means. **Added bars shall have development length ≥ 20 bar diameters (20d_b)** on each side; **if plain round bars are used, ends shall be hooked**.
  3. Thermal stresses from heat of hydration, internal/external temperature changes, drying shrinkage cracking and other stresses shall be considered in choosing joint location and form.

#### 9.1.1 Horizontal construction joints (Construction of horizontal construction joints) [REQ]
1. The joint line should be truly horizontal (level).
2. When new concrete is placed on existing concrete, the old surface shall be cleaned free of laitance (ฝ้าน้ำปูน) and loose concrete, and shall be moist (wet).
3. Before placing, forms shall be verified as sufficiently stable during placing; concrete shall be adequately compacted to ensure firm bond of old and new concrete.
4. Where new concrete is placed **beneath** existing concrete, verify that mix and method are suitable so the joint bonds tightly after settlement of the fresh concrete.
- **[REC] (p.114–115)**
  1. For appearance, keep joint lines straight — align with form panel edges, or mark the joint level on the form and fill exactly to that mark.
  2. For strong, durable, watertight joints, the first lift must be well placed, non-segregated and fully cured. Any laitance, weak concrete or loose aggregate on the surface must be removed before placing new concrete.
     - Surface preparation methods: pre-hardening processing (เตรียมผิวก่อนคอนกรีตแข็งตัว, green-cut) and post-hardening processing (เตรียมผิวหลังคอนกรีตแข็งตัว), or both.
     - Pre-hardening: high-pressure air-and-water jet to strip the surface of concrete that is placed but not fully hardened, exposing coarse aggregate to create a rough surface — very effective for large joints. Risk of damage if timing/procedure is wrong → plan carefully; a set retarder may be applied to the joint surface zone.
     - Post-hardening: high-pressure air/water jet if surface not very hard; if very hard, wire-brush or sandblast (Sandblasting), then wash clean with water.
  3. (p.115) Form ties/anchors should be placed close to the forms to hold the forms tight before placing; prevent mortar from leaking onto the prepared old surface or into the joint line.
     - When placing against old concrete, a thick cement grout or mortar should be applied on the old surface before placing new concrete to improve bond. Mortar is most effective when the new concrete has low slump and relatively large aggregate. **The w/c (or w/binder) ratio of the joint mortar should be lower than that of the concrete.**
  4. (p.115) Where new concrete is placed below old concrete, bonding is difficult because the new concrete bleeds at its top and settles, so its top does not bond to the old concrete. Use one of the methods in Fig 9.1, or use an expansive admixture (สารผสมเพิ่มเพื่อขยายตัว, Expansive admixture) in the new concrete.

**Figure 9.1 — Placing fresh concrete beneath existing concrete (รูปที่ 9.1)** (p.115): three methods shown in section:
- *Direct method (วิธีการเทโดยตรง)*: underside of old concrete is sloped/V-shaped; new concrete is placed through a side hopper/form extension up to a level **above** the underside of the old concrete (label "ความสูงเหนือคอนกรีตเดิม" – head above old concrete). [REC] Old-concrete joint surface usually sloped to one side or V-shaped (lets air and bleed water escape); new concrete should have low air and low settlement, and be well vibrated.
- *Filling method (วิธีการเติม)*: new concrete placed nearly to the joint, then the remaining gap filled with mortar ("เติมมอร์ต้าร์") containing an expansion aid or aluminium powder; forms must resist mortar expansion pressure; remove laitance/bleed-water film from both the new and old surfaces before placing the mortar.
- *Injection method (วิธีฉีด)*: used alone or with the direct method; cement grout (with expansive admixture) injected through pipes pre-embedded at the joint ("Grouting").

#### 9.1.2 Vertical construction joints (Construction of vertical construction joints) [REQ] (p.116)
1. Stop-end forms for vertical joints shall be firm and rigid; concrete around the joint shall be adequately consolidated by vibrator.
2. The old concrete surface shall be moist and coated with thick cement grout, mortar, or epoxy; and shall be roughened — e.g., wire brushing or chipping (กระเทาะ).
3. New concrete should be well compacted for maximum bond; re-vibration of the new concrete at the appropriate time is also recommended.
- **[REC]**
  1. Stop-ends are usually simple forms with holes for bars; must be strong enough for vibration without damage or mortar leakage. One method: use **wire mesh (ลวดตาข่าย) of 5 mm opening** as the stop-end, left embedded in the concrete, supported by a frame of reinforcing bars (Fig 9.2).
  2. Sandblasting is good for roughening old concrete; if not very hard, wire brushing is acceptable. After roughening, coat immediately with cement paste, mortar or epoxy just before placing new concrete.
  3. (p.117) Freshly placed concrete at the joint tends to separate due to bleeding → compact by re-vibration (Revibration) at the proper time to reduce bleeding and improve the joint. The proper time is the latest time after placing at which the concrete is still plastic and can be re-vibrated without damage.

**Figure 9.2 — Vertical joint using wire mesh (รูปที่ 9.2)** (p.116): elevation and plan showing wire mesh stop-end supported by reinforcement framing, old concrete one side, new concrete the other; bars pass through the mesh.

#### 9.1.3 Construction joints in columns and walls integrated with floor systems [REQ] (p.117)
1. Construction joints in columns or walls connected to the floor system should be made continuous around the floor-system zone (i.e., joint located so column/wall concrete is continuous through the floor connection region — the text says "recommended").
2. Concrete in haunches (Haunch) should be placed monolithically with the floor; cantilevers (Cantilever) should likewise be placed continuously throughout.
- **[REC]**
  1. **Where specified column concrete strength exceeds 1.4 × the floor-system concrete strength**, load transfer through the lower-strength floor concrete shall be handled by one of:
     - Place concrete of the column strength in the floor at the column location, **extending over an area 4 × the column area**, cast monolithically (puddling / "mushroom").
     - In computing the strength of the column through the floor, use the lower concrete strength, and provide dowels and ties as required.
     - For columns laterally supported on all four sides by slabs or beams of approximately equal depth, use an effective concrete strength = **75 % of column concrete strength + 35 % of floor concrete strength** (0.75 f'c,col + 0.35 f'c,slab).
  2. Concrete at enlargements such as column capitals (Column capital) or drop panels (Drop panel) should be placed with the floor since they are part of the floor; they then do not shrink (Shrink) or settle (Settle) with the column concrete and behave as floor.

#### 9.1.4 Construction joints of floor systems (Construction joints of floor systems) [REQ] (p.117–118)
- Construction joints in RC floor systems should be located **at mid-span of the slab or beam**. Where a joist (ตง, Joist) frames into a beam at mid-span, the beam's joint should be offset from the joist by **twice the joist width** (clause text: "สองเท่าของความกว้างของตง"; commentary says twice the joist thickness; Fig 9.3 shows "> 2b₀" with b₀ = joist width). Shear reinforcement in the beam should be provided to resist diagonal tension (Diagonal tension) across this joint.
- **[REC]**
  - Mid-span has low shear and compression stresses normal to the joint, so the joint has little effect on strength. Offset from the joist ≥ 2 × joist dimension avoids abrupt change of stress level (Level of stress); shear is high at such a joint → add bars inclined at **45° to horizontal** across the joint (Fig 9.3).
  - **Prestressed concrete:** joint should be at **L/5 to L/3 from the support centreline** (L = centre-to-centre span of supports), straight and perpendicular to the plane of the slab.

**Figure 9.3 — Shear reinforcement at a construction joint (รูปที่ 9.3)** (p.118): beam elevation; joist (ตง) of width b₀ at mid-span (℄); construction joint located **more than 2b₀** from the joist face; hooked bent-up bars crossing the joint at **45°**.

#### 9.1.5 Construction joints of an arch (Construction joints of an arch) [REQ]
1. Joints in arches shall be perpendicular (normal) to the arch axis.
2. If the arch is sufficiently wide, joints may be made parallel to the span direction.

### 9.2 Expansion joints (รอยต่อเผื่อขยาย, Expansion joints) [REQ] (p.118–119)
- The two parts of the structure should be completely separated. If necessary, fill the joint with joint filler (วัสดุอุดรอย, Joint filler), water stop (แผ่นกันน้ำ, Water stop) or other material.
- **[REC]**
  - Complete separation may or may not be adopted depending on member type and joint location; concrete should be completely separated, but reinforcement may be continuous.
  - If a completely separated joint might not form along the intended line, use a key/tongue, groove, or bars unbonded at one end to obtain the desired joint (Fig 9.4).
  - Debris may enter the gap → pre-fill with joint filler. For watertight expansion joints use a water stop of suitable flexibility.

**Figure 9.4 — Types of expansion joints (รูปที่ 9.4)** (p.119):
- (1)–(4): expansion joints for walls — (1) plain butt joint filled with bitumen/other between concrete; (2) tongue-and-groove (keyed); (3) plain butt/offset; (4) with reinforcing bar (dowel) across joint.
- (5)–(6): watertight expansion joints for walls or slab-type structures — water stop + joint filler + waterproof sealant (วัสดุอุดกันน้ำ); (6) a stepped/lapped slab joint with sealant, filler and water stop.
- (7)–(8): expansion joints under water tanks — (7) with bituminous membrane and joint filler; (8) with neoprene ring and joint filler.
- (9): Mesnager hinge (crossed bars).
(No numeric dimensions shown.)

### 9.3 Crack-control joints (รอยต่อเผื่อแตก, Crack-control joints) [REQ] (p.120)
- Crack-control joints shall be at suitable locations and shall not damage the structure.
- **[REC]** Deformation from hydration temperature differentials, ambient conditions, drying shrinkage, loading, etc., cracks concrete if it cannot move freely → provide joints to force cracks to occur at predetermined locations. In water-retaining structures, include a water stop in the crack-control joint (Fig 9.5).

**Figure 9.5 — Examples of crack-control joints (รูปที่ 9.5)** (p.120, units mm):
- *Fig ก (a)*: wall of height H on a base, with crack-control joints (vertical grooves) at intervals ("ระยะระหว่างรอยต่อเผื่อแตก" spacing, no value given). Groove detail: V-notches on both faces each **0.1d** deep, wall thickness d.
  - (1)–(4) crack-inducing joints (รอยต่อเผื่อแตก): (1) section depth d reduced so remaining section = **0.8d** (i.e., notches totaling 0.2d), crack forms at notch; (2) same with water stop at mid-thickness; (3) embedded pipe (ท่อ) at centre: **0.4d / 0.4d** either side of pipe, with notches; (4) crossed water stop at centre.
  - (5)–(6) shear-transfer joints (รอยต่อรับแรงเฉือน): (5) precast concrete insert (คอนกรีตหล่อสำเร็จ) with releasing agent; (6) split PVC pipe (ท่อ PVC ผ่าซีก) with releasing agent.
  - Example notch details (ตัวอย่างรอยบากเผื่อแตก): triangular notch depth **30–40**; trapezoidal notch **40** deep, **40** top, **50** base.
- *Fig ข (b)*: wall section of thickness W: surface groove each face, **70 wide at inner / 100 wide at outer** (trapezoid), depth **0.1–0.15W**; steel plate (crack inducer) of thickness **0.6–1.2 mm**; water stop **300 mm wide**; reinforcement continuous.

## 10. Formwork and shoring (แบบหล่อคอนกรีตและค้ำยัน) — pp.121–130

### 10.1 General [REQ]
- Formwork (แบบหล่อ) and shoring (ค้ำยัน) shall be designed and constructed strong enough to resist forces from placing and vibration, and stiff enough that finished concrete is within tolerance (Tolerance).
- **[REC]**
  1. Excavated earth shall not be used as vertical formwork unless approved by the engineer.
  2. Formwork and shoring shop drawings shall be submitted to the engineer for approval before use, where the specification so requires.

### 10.2 Design loads (แรงที่ใช้ออกแบบ) [REQ]
Formwork and shoring shall be designed for the following, considering actual working conditions:
- **10.2.1 Vertical loads:** weight of concrete, reinforcement, formwork, shoring, workers, tools, machinery and other temporary items, plus impact.
- **10.2.2 Horizontal loads:** vibration of concrete, impact, wind, flowing-water pressure, earthquake, effects of erection inaccuracy (out-of-plumb).
- **10.2.3 Fresh concrete pressure:** forms shall be designed for pressure of fresh concrete, which behaves like a liquid.
- **10.2.4 Other loads:** loads that may occur during construction.
- **[REC]**
  1. Normal design unit weights: **concrete 2,400 kg/m³** and **reinforcement 150 kg/m³ of concrete**. Workers, tools and other equipment: **60 to 250 kg/m²** depending on number/weight of equipment.
  2. Formwork/shoring failures may result from horizontal loads (vibration, impact, eccentricity). Design horizontal load **not less than 150 kg/m (per metre along the edge) or 2 % of total dead load**, applied at the **top of the shoring**.
  3. Lateral pressure of fresh concrete depends on mix and consistency, rate and height of placing, vibration method, concrete temperature, admixtures, member size/shape, and reinforcement amount. Approximate formulae:

  **Columns (แบบหล่อเสา):**
  > P = 800 + 80,000 R / (T + 20)  kg/m²  …(10.1)
  > P ≤ 15,000 kg/m² and ≤ 2,400 H kg/m²

  **Walls (แบบหล่อกำแพง):**
  - Rate of placing **< 2 m/h**:
  > P = 800 + 80,000 R / (T + 20)  kg/m²  …(10.2)
  > P ≤ 10,000 kg/m² and ≤ 2,400 H kg/m²
  - Rate of placing **> 2 m/h**:
  > P = 800 + (120,000 + 25,000 R) / (T + 20)  kg/m²  …(10.3)
  > P ≤ 10,000 kg/m² and ≤ 2,400 H kg/m²

  where P = lateral fresh-concrete pressure (kg/m²); R = rate of placing, measured vertically (m/h); T = concrete temperature (°C); H = height of fresh concrete in the form (m).

  (Office note, not in the standard: 1 kg/m² ≈ 9.81 Pa, so 15,000 kg/m² ≈ 147 kPa and 10,000 kg/m² ≈ 98 kPa; the formulae follow the form of the older ACI 347 formulae.)

  - For very fluid concrete, e.g., self-compacting (flowing) concrete not requiring vibration, compute lateral pressure as full liquid head: **P = 2,400 H kg/m²**.
  4. "Other loads" means local loads (Local load) such as asymmetric placing, placing on sloping/unlevel forms, uplift (flotation) of pipes embedded in the concrete, etc.

### 10.3 Design calculations (การคำนวณออกแบบ) [REQ]
- Design and construction of formwork and shoring is the **contractor's responsibility**. For important structures, drawings and calculations shall be submitted to the engineer for approval before use. Design shall resist the loads of 10.2.

#### 10.3.1 Formwork design [REQ]
1. Formwork shall produce concrete of size and shape within specified tolerances, accounting for form deflection and shore shortening/settlement or buckling.
2. **Deflection of formwork faces visible in the finished concrete shall not exceed 1/240 of the span between main supporting members.**
3. Formwork shall have no gaps allowing mortar leakage; all sharp (exposed) corners of concrete members should be chamfered (ลบมุม) unless prohibited by the specification.
4. Temporary openings should be provided at the bottom of column and wall forms for cleaning/inspection before placing.
5. Wedges for adjusting form level/position shall be capable of being fixed tight after final inspection before placing.
- **[REC]** (p.124)
  1. Forms should have ties or bracing to hold shape and position under concrete pressure.
  2. Forms should be designed for easy and safe erection and stripping without vibration or impact to the concrete.
  3. Chamfering prevents corner damage during stripping, eases work and improves appearance.
  4. Bottom cleanouts in column/wall forms; for tall forms provide temporary placing windows at suitable levels to avoid segregation during placing.

#### 10.3.2 Shoring design (การคำนวณออกแบบค้ำยัน) [REQ]
1. Shoring shall transfer all loads to the ground or to supporting foundations with deflection/settlement within specified limits.
2. Shoring shall be easy to erect and remove; all shore joints/splices shall be adequately strong and not prone to buckling.
3. Adequate diagonal bracing of shores should be provided for formwork stability.
4. Shore pins/bolts or level-adjusting wedges shall be capable of being fixed tight after final inspection before placing.
5. The ground or base supporting shores shall not deflect/settle excessively such that the finished concrete exceeds tolerances.
- **[REC]** (p.124–125)
  1. Guard against buckling failure; use proper diagonal bracing; design shoring for horizontal loads by bracing or tying shore tops to strong existing concrete structure.
  2. Shores should be removable and erectable easily and safely without impact, using jacks or wedges.
  3. Provide beams/bracing to redistribute load to other shores if any shore foundation settles differentially (Differential settlement).
  4. For proprietary/patented shores or splicing systems, follow the manufacturer's load-capacity recommendations strictly, including bracing and safe load for unbraced length.
  5. **Field lap splices of shores: not more than every other shore under slabs, or not more than every third shore under beams; not more than one splice per shore unless bracing is provided at every splice.** Splices distributed as uniformly as possible; splices shall not be near the mid-height of an unbraced shore or midway between lateral supports (to prevent buckling).
  6. Splices shall be designed against buckling and bending like other compression members; **splice material for timber shores shall be not shorter than 1 m**.

### 10.4 Erection of formwork and shoring (การประกอบติดตั้งแบบหล่อและค้ำยัน) [REQ] (p.125)
1. Formwork and shoring shall be fastened tight and rigid with pins, wedges or bolts as designed.
2. Formwork shall be inspected before placing reinforcement.
3. Formwork shall be cleaned before every use; form faces should be coated with release agent (น้ำยาถอดแบบ) for easy stripping.
4. Erection should include camber/allowance to compensate for deflection of forms, shores or shore foundations predicted by calculation.
5. Before placing, clean out foreign debris; forms should have cleanout openings, especially at the bottom of column and wall forms.
- **[REC]**
  1. When erecting on young concrete, take care not to crack it.
  2. Avoid stockpiling heavy materials on forms or stacking forms on each other (damage).
  3. Release agent should be tested (or have test results) showing no adverse effects, e.g., on compressive strength, or residue preventing paint adhesion; do not over-apply so as to contaminate reinforcement.

### 10.5 Removal of formwork and shoring (การถอดแบบหล่อและค้ำยัน) [REQ] (p.126)
1. Forms and shores may be removed only when the concrete has sufficient compressive strength to carry its own weight and other construction loads.
2. Sequence and timing of removal depend on cement properties, mix proportions, importance of the structure, type/size of structure, loads, temperature, etc.
3. For general structures without specified requirements, forms/shores may be removed when field-cured specimens (cured the same way as the structure) reach the minimum compressive strength of Table 10.1.
4. For general structures without specified requirements and **without compressive-strength test results**, use the earliest stripping times of Table 10.2.

**Table 10.1 — Minimum concrete compressive strength for removal of forms and shores, general structures (ตารางที่ 10.1)**

| Formwork type | Minimum concrete compressive strength (MPa) |
|---|---|
| Side forms of columns, beams, walls and footings | 5 |
| Soffit forms of slabs and beams (incl. shores) | 14 |

**Table 10.2 — Minimum concrete age for removal of forms and shores, general structures (ตารางที่ 10.2)**

| Formwork type | Minimum concrete age (days) |
|---|---|
| Side forms of columns, beams, walls and footings | 2 |
| Soffit forms of slabs and beams | 14 |

- **[REC]** (p.127)
  1. Removal sequence should ensure the partially shored structure can carry resulting forces/moments without cracking.
  2. Stockpiling material on the structure after shore removal shall be checked as not hazardous — structure may not yet carry design loads; material stockpiled before shore removal may need to be moved if removal would endanger the structure.

### 10.6 Reshoring (การค้ำยันกลับ, Reshoring) [REQ] (p.127)
- Definition: removing forms and shores from a concrete structure and then reinstalling shores.
1. Reshoring to support structural loads shall be planned in advance and **approved by the engineer** only.
2. A structure awaiting reshoring shall not carry live load unless checked not to exceed its current capacity.
3. During reshoring the structure shall not carry loads greater than specified by the engineer. Reshoring shall be done **as soon as possible** after stripping.
4. Reshores shall be tightened to carry the specified loads, and shall remain in place **until compressive-strength test results reach the specified criterion**.
- **[REC]**
  1. Purpose: let newly stripped concrete carry loads and/or transfer additional loads to the structure below.
  2. Cases: stripping forms for reuse elsewhere and reshoring to carry later loads; or when later loads exceed original form capacity — strip and reshore with stronger shores.

### 10.8 Special formwork (แบบหล่อชนิดพิเศษ) (p.129–130)

#### 10.8.1 Slipform (แบบหล่อเคลื่อนที่, Slipform) [REQ]
1. Designed for loads of 10.2 plus friction between form and concrete.
2. Movement shall be continuous until the structure is completed or the specified level is reached.
3. Rate of slip shall suit concrete quality and construction conditions; concrete emerging from the slipform shall have sufficient compressive strength to carry all loads imposed on it.

#### 10.8.2 Movable formwork and falsework (นั่งร้านเคลื่อนที่ได้, Movable formwork and falsework) [REQ]
- Comprises forms, supporting beams, supporting posts and devices allowing horizontal and/or vertical movement.
1. Shall be designed strong, safe and with adequate load capacity.
2. Where loads are transferred through it to the concrete structure, verify by calculation that the concrete can carry them in every case.
3. Shall be inspected regularly after erection and during operation to ensure safety and capacity.
4. Shall be designed to operate without damaging the concrete, during erection and operation.
5. Shall be provided with adequate camber (Camber) for settlement and deflection.

## 11. Surface finishing (การตกแต่งผิวหน้าคอนกรีต) — pp.131–133

### 11.1 General [REQ]
- For attractive, uniform surfaces, place continuously using concrete of the same mix proportions, same type of materials and same placing method.
- **[REC]** Discontinuous placing creates joints; where joints are necessary, plan them to be straight lines for appearance.

### 11.2 Finishing of unformed surfaces (ผิวที่ไม่ได้ใช้แบบหล่อ) [REQ]
- Unformed surfaces normally = horizontal surfaces.
1. After compacting and screeding to level and shape, wait until bleed water has evaporated or been removed before finishing; do not over-finish (too much or too long).
2. Cracks forming on the finished surface can be removed by recompaction or refinishing before the concrete begins to set.
3. For a smooth, dense surface, press a (steel) trowel over the entire surface.
4. Finishing should be avoided while it is raining.
- **[REC]**
  1. Finishing with bleed water present causes many fine surface cracks (crazing); over-finishing brings excess paste to the surface leading to surface cracking.
  2. Cracks before setting may be from plastic shrinkage (Plastic shrinkage) or settlement (Settlement); settlement cracks follow the reinforcement lines.
  3. Final troweling should be done when the surface has stiffened moderately — test by finger pressure; the right time is when a finger press no longer leaves an impression.
  4. Rain washes fresh surfaces, reducing surface mortar → stop finishing until rain stops, or provide fully effective rain cover.
  5. **Roof slabs, including canopies and roof decks, should not be given a smooth (hard-troweled/polished) finish unless specified on drawings.**

### 11.3 Finishing of formed surfaces (ผิวที่ใช้แบบหล่อ) [REQ]
1. For exposed (fair-faced) concrete (คอนกรีตเปลือย), placing and compaction shall be controlled to give a surface fully covered by mortar.
2. Fins/projections should be removed to give a smooth surface; honeycomb or cracks should be repaired with mortar or concrete of suitable mix: chip out unsound concrete, wet with water, then repair with prepared mortar/concrete.
3. Severe cracking from shrinkage or temperature differentials shall be repaired by a suitable method.
- **[REC]**
  1. After stripping, a good surface is mortar-covered with no clearly visible aggregate, except where exposed-aggregate finish is designed.
  2. Special finishes like brushing, rubbing, grinding or plastering may be done after stripping once the concrete has moderate strength; terrazzo grinding, bush-hammering, or sandblasting only after the concrete has fully hardened. To fill pores and bugholes: wet the surface thoroughly, then rub in a mix of **1 part cement : 2.5 parts fine sand** with a finishing tool.
  3. Some cracks affect structural capacity — repair considering necessity and appropriate method; repairs should not interfere with curing.

### 11.4 Abrasion-resistant surfaces (ผิวที่รับแรงขัดสี) [REQ]
- For high abrasion resistance use concrete with low water/cement or water/binder ratio, compact thoroughly, and cure adequately.
- **[REC]** Applies to road pavements, dam surfaces, spillways, water conduits, etc. Also use high-strength aggregate, good placing/compaction, curing as long as practicable; or special concretes such as polymer concrete or steel-fibre-reinforced concrete. (No numeric w/c limit given.)

### 11.5 Special finishes (ผิวชนิดพิเศษ) [REQ]
- Any special finish shall not adversely affect the structural capacity of the concrete.
- **[REC]** Examples: grinding, painting, washing, coating, sandblasting, chipping/bush-hammering, tiling or cladding, sprayed mortar coating. Must not reduce strength or capacity, e.g., by reducing section dimensions or removing concrete.

## 12. Quality control and inspection (การควบคุมคุณภาพและการตรวจสอบ) — PDF p.135

### 12.1 General (ทั่วไป) — p.135
- **[REQ]** Concrete, reinforcement, machinery, equipment and labour used in concrete work shall be controlled so that the structure is strong, safe, economical and durable.
- **[REC]** A good overall QC system is needed covering concrete, reinforcement, machinery/equipment and labour. QC of concrete shall be agreed and mutually understood among the planner, designer, construction supervisor (ผู้ควบคุมงาน), contractor and construction inspector **before construction starts**. Required tests are defined in 12.2. If test results are doubtful, a suitable remedy should be found immediately. Excessive testing merely to gather more data is **not** good QC practice.

### 12.2 Testing (การทดสอบ) — p.135

#### 12.2.1 Test methods (วิธีการทดสอบ)
- **[REQ]** Use test methods specified in accepted concrete testing standards.
- **[REC]** Use Thai Industrial Standards (TIS / มอก.) test methods; where a method is not covered by TIS, foreign standards may be used, e.g. **ASTM, BS or JIS**.

#### 12.2.2 Tests for concrete (การทดสอบสำหรับคอนกรีต)
- **12.2.2.1 [REQ]** Tests of the constituent materials shall be done **before** mix proportioning is calculated; tests needed for batching/mixing shall be done **before** mixing.
- **12.2.2.2 [REQ]** The following tests should be carried out (p.136):
  1. Aggregate properties (คุณสมบัติของมวลรวม)
  2. Slump (ค่ายุบตัว)
  3. Air content (ปริมาณอากาศ)
  4. Bulk density / unit weight of concrete (น้ำหนักต่อหน่วยปริมาตร)
  5. Compressive strength (กำลังรับแรงอัด)
  6. Chloride content of fresh concrete (ปริมาณคลอไรด์ในคอนกรีตสด)
  7. Other necessary tests
- **12.2.2.3 [REQ]** Field-cured specimens (cured on site under the same conditions as the structure) should be tested **in parallel with** standard-cured specimens, to assess adequacy of curing, appropriate time for form removal or prestressing, or safety when loading at early age.
- **12.2.2.4 [REQ]** Where necessary, non-destructive tests (การทดสอบแบบไม่ทำลาย, NDT) or tests on cores drilled from the finished structure (แท่งตัวอย่างที่เจาะ) may be required after completion.

**[REC] commentary to 12.2.2 (p.136–138):**
- (1) Before proportioning and mixing, test materials to select suitable materials/mix — e.g. chloride content of sea sand; potential alkali–aggregate reactivity (reactive aggregate). On large projects also test for **consistency/uniformity** of material quality, not just compliance.
  - Chloride in concrete shall be computed as the **sum from all sources** (aggregates, mixing water, chemical admixtures, etc.); total shall not exceed the allowable chloride in the mix. Correlate calculated total with measured values for analysis.
  - Before work starts, confirm all construction plant and equipment perform to the required standard.
- (2) Test materials during construction for quality and uniformity:
  - **Aggregate tests:** verify properties; if grading or moisture changes, adjust to keep concrete uniform. At the start of construction, test aggregate **grading and moisture at least 2 times per day**; grading-test frequency may be reduced if grading is found not to vary.
  - **Slump:** indicates workability; normally tested on site before placing; for some work also tested at the batch plant before dispatch for initial plant QC. If slump changes during work, re-test constituent materials.
  - **Air content:** needed where air entrainment is used for freeze–thaw resistance (e.g. cold-storage rooms / ห้องเย็น) to confirm the required air content.
  - **Bulk density:** needed particularly for lightweight concrete, heavyweight concrete, and floating structures, where density is controlled.
  - **Compressive strength:** key quality index; tensile, flexural and shear strengths can usually be estimated from it. Loading method and specimen shape/size affect results. The **28-day standard cylinder** strength is normally the design reference value. Early-age tests needed e.g. for precast members or determining form-stripping time; for concrete with large pozzolan content, test also at **56 or 91 days**. Report results to all concerned as quickly as possible.
  - **Chloride in fresh concrete:** QC test since chloride comes from several sources (aggregates, water, admixtures); total chloride in fresh concrete shall not exceed the value in **clause 1.3**.
  - **Other tests:** fresh-concrete analysis (mix proportion or strength prediction before placing), core strength tests and NDT (strength/durability of completed structure). Sampling for these should be done carefully so specimens truly represent the problem investigated.
- (3) Compressive strength is needed to assess curing time/method, form removal time, prestressing time, and safety under load; especially in very cold or very hot weather, **field-cured** specimens must also be tested.
- (4) After completion, if concrete quality is in doubt, check by core compressive strength or NDT; in some cases use **more than one method** to increase confidence, since one method alone may not reliably assess strength/quality.
- (No numerical core acceptance criteria, number of cores, core size, or NDT method/standards are given in this standard.)

#### 12.2.3 Tests of reinforcement (การทดสอบเหล็กเสริม) — p.138
- **[REQ]** (1) Reinforcement shall be tested to ensure quality before use.
- **[REQ]** (2) Splices/couplers and welded joints (ข้อต่อและรอยเชื่อมต่อ) shall be strength-tested before use.
- **[REC]** (1) Reinforcement must meet design specification properties → tensile tests required; for deformed bars also check deformation (rib/lug) shape and dimensions. Sampling and site testing per **TIS 20 (มอก. 20)** for round bars and **TIS 24 (มอก. 24)** for deformed bars. (Years not stated in this clause.)
- **[REC]** (2) Splice/weld tests ensure joint strength is not less than the unspliced bar. Per **EIT 1007 (วสท. 1007) clause 3405**: a proper **welded splice shall develop in tension at least 125 % of the specified yield strength** of the bar (≥ 1.25 fy); a proper **mechanical (end-anchorage/coupler) splice** shall be designed to carry critical tension or compression equivalent to a proper welded splice. Joint quality depends on splice/weld type, materials, workmanship and method. Test method must suit the structure type — where joints carry shear as well as tension, test shear strength of the joint too.
  - Splices/welds should **not all be located at the same section** even when tests show joint strength exceeds the bar, because workmanship varies and co-located splices cause stress concentration.
  - Splice/weld tests per TIS or accepted foreign standards (**ASTM, BS, JIS**).

#### 12.2.4 Reporting of test results (การรายงานผลการทดสอบ) — p.139
- **[REQ]** All test results shall be reported **immediately** to the engineer concerned.
- **[REC]** The supervising engineer controls/instructs work using test results, e.g. may order mix changes if strength does not meet the specification; hence results must be sent without delay.

### 12.3 Quality control of concrete (การควบคุมคุณภาพของคอนกรีต) — p.139

#### 12.3.1 QC using compressive strength
- **[REQ]** (1) QC by compressive strength normally uses **28-day** strength; specimens shall be as representative as possible of concrete placed in the structure.
- **[REQ]** (2) One strength test result = **average of 3 specimens**.
- **[REQ]** (3) Sampling for compressive strength: generally **at least once per day, or at least once per every 50 m³ (ลบ.ม.) of continuous placement**.
- **[REQ]** (4) Control charts/graphs of test results should be kept.

**[REC] (p.140):**
- (1) 28-day standard-cylinder strength is the design value, so 28-day testing during construction is essential. Early-age tests are also needed to predict/confirm strength before 28 days so anomalies can be corrected promptly. Prediction methods: **accelerated hydration** (hot-water curing, prediction within **2 hours to 2 days**) — requires established correlation with normally cured 28-day strength; or prediction from **3-day or 7-day** strength — requires established 3/7-day vs 28-day correlation and good control of casting, curing conditions and curing temperature. Predictions only detect trends; **28-day strength testing is always still required**.
- (2) Strength depends on materials, casting workmanship, curing and testing skill → each result should be the mean of **3 specimens**.
- (3) Sampling frequency must adequately indicate uniformity; too few samples → wrong assessment; many samples improve QC but cost time/labour. Frequency may be reduced if results are consistent and there is no change of raw materials or mix.
- (4) Control charts speed up analysis. Charts should show clear **lower and upper limit lines**, plus curing temperature, mix number, date/time of placement and other data to help trace causes. Charts are also useful for slump, unit weight and air content.

### 12.4 Quality assessment / acceptance of concrete (การตรวจสอบคุณภาพของคอนกรีต) — p.141

- **12.4.1 [REQ]** Assess test results both **overall** and **by successive periods/lots** (ช่วง ๆ).
- **12.4.2 [REQ]** By compressive strength, concrete is acceptable only when analysis shows the **probability of test results falling below the specified strength is less than 5 %**. Normally 28-day strength is used. Sampling generally **not less than 1 per day or 1 per 50 m³ of continuous placement**. Each test result should be the **average of 3 specimens**.
- **12.4.3 [REQ]** Where water–cement ratio (อัตราส่วนน้ำต่อปูนซีเมนต์) or water–binder ratio (อัตราส่วนน้ำต่อวัสดุประสาน) is checked to assess chemical resistance or watertightness (ความทึบน้ำ), concrete is acceptable only when the **mean measured w/c (w/b) is lower than the required w/c (w/b)**.
- **12.4.4 [REQ]** If concrete being placed is found non-conforming: change/adjust the mix, or check efficiency of equipment and working methods in production/placing; at the same time check whether concrete already placed meets requirements, and **remedy if not**.

**[REC] commentary (p.141–145):**
- (1) Quality varies batch-to-batch and day-to-day (materials, workmanship, conditions, equipment). Within-day variation is normally smaller than between-day, especially on long projects with discontinuous placing → evaluate by period as well as overall, to prevent continuous production of poor concrete over many batches. Too few samples may cause larger volumes of concrete to be rejected; too many waste labour/cost.
- (2) Strength requirement is in **clause 3.2 (design strength)**. Acceptance assumes test results are **normally distributed** and probability below specified strength **< 5 %**.

  **Acceptance by coefficient of acceptability k (ค่าสัมประสิทธิ์ความยอมรับได้)** (verified from page image p.142):

  - Eq.(1): x̄ ≥ f'c + k·Sn
  - x̄ = mean compressive strength of tested specimens (MPa) = (x1 + x2 + … + xn)/n
  - f'c = specified compressive strength of concrete (MPa)
  - Sn = standard deviation (ค่าความเบี่ยงเบนมาตรฐาน) (MPa)
  - Eq.(2): Sn = √[ (x1² + x2² + … + xn² − n·x̄²) / (n − 1) ]
  - Eq.(3): k = (x̄ − f'c) / Sn
  - Eq.(4): for producer's risk α = 0.05 (normal distribution table), k = |(x̄ − f'c)/Sn| = **1.65**.
  - Text states "k shall have a value **not exceeding 1.65**" (ไม่เกิน 1.65). NOTE: this wording is inconsistent with Eq.(1); the intended criterion for acceptance is x̄ ≥ f'c + 1.65·Sn, i.e. k ≥ 1.65 (probability < 5 % below f'c). Recommend General Notes state: **x̄ ≥ f'c + 1.65 Sn**.

  - **Fig. 12.1** "Normal distribution curves with different standard deviations" (p.143): x-axis compressive strength 130–410 (labelled "MPa (เมกาปาสกาล)" but the range corresponds to ksc-type values, i.e. an apparent unit-label error in the figure), y-axis percent of tests (0–25 %). A vertical line marks **Specified Strength f'c at ≈230**; three normal curves with increasing spread have their means at ≈245, ≈260 and ≈290 respectively, showing that for the same k, wider scatter (higher Sn) requires a higher mean strength → poorer control efficiency.

  **Process capability index (ดัชนีความสามารถของกระบวนการผลิต)** alternative for industrial QC, controlling only the lower limit of strength:
  - Eq.(5): C_PL = (x̄ − f'c) / (3·Sn)
  - Eq.(6): with α = 0.05, C_PL = 1.65·Sn / (3·Sn) = **0.55**
  - → **C_PL shall be not less than 0.55**.

  - Concrete complying with **Chapter 5 "Ready-mixed concrete" (คอนกรีตผสมเสร็จ)** may be deemed acceptable per Eq.(4).
  - With few strength results (minor or small structures), also examine **individual specimen results** against the specified strength.
  - Reliable Sn needs sufficient data; for few data, adjust Sn per **ACI (ACI Manual of Concrete Inspection)**, or adjust the coefficient of variation per **JSCE (Standard Specification for Design and Construction of Concrete Structures – 1986, Part 2)**, or other standards. (No numerical modification factors are tabulated in this standard.)
  - Basis for all above: **28-day standard-cured cylinder** strength; 28-day strength may be estimated from early-age strength if enough correlation data exist.
- (3) For concrete designed by w/c or w/b for chemical resistance/watertightness: check by analysis of fresh concrete w/c (w/b) or by estimating w/c (w/b) from hardened-concrete strength; acceptable when the value obtained is **lower than the design w/c (w/b)**.
- (4) Actions when results do not conform:
  - (4.1) Uncertainty due to insufficient data at project start: check each operation step; work may continue with caution while checking equipment, plant, labour, batching, mixing, transporting, placing etc., and improve.
  - (4.2) Uncertainty during placing: improve mix proportions, strength, raw-material quality, batching equipment/method, transport and techniques; also test **cores**, **NDT** or other tests to confirm in-place concrete; temporary measures e.g. **extend curing period**.
  - (4.3) Usual causes: poor materials, poor mix design, poor production, poor specimen preparation, poor testing. If not due to specimen preparation/testing, immediate remedies: raise target (required) strength or reduce allowable variability (implying better materials and production). If the structure was built with non-conforming concrete, **strengthening** must be specified; otherwise demolition may result.

### 12.5 Structural inspection and load testing (การตรวจสอบโครงสร้างและการทดสอบการรับน้ำหนักของโครงสร้าง) — p.145

#### 12.5.1 Inspection of structure (การตรวจสอบโครงสร้าง)
- **[REQ]** The structure should be inspected after completion of construction.
- **[REC]** Purpose: verify dimensions, shape, position, materials and details comply with drawings, and detect defects or undesirable cracking. Any such findings must be remedied per the responsible engineer's instructions **before** the structure is permitted to be used.

#### 12.5.2 Load testing of completed structure (การทดสอบการรับน้ำหนักของโครงสร้างที่ก่อสร้างเสร็จแล้ว)
- **[REQ]** (1) Choose test method to suit the purpose; test method and load magnitude shall not harm the structure.
- **[REQ]** (2) During and after testing, **stresses, deflections (การแอ่นตัว) or other deformations** caused by the test load **shall not exceed the values estimated in design**.
- **[REQ]** (3) If testing shows load capacity or durability is inadequate relative to design, the structure **shall be strengthened** by suitable means.
- **[REC] (p.145–146):**
  - Load tests normally done when safety is in doubt — e.g. in-place strength may be lower than specimen strength due to sudden heavy rain during construction, or cracks appear after completion.
  - Test only after the structure has adequate strength (all parts at full age); **normally not before 56 days age**, to avoid failure of under-strength parts.
  - Normally only **flexural members (slabs, beams)** are load tested; **columns should not be load tested**.
  - Load testing may be needed where new construction methods or new materials are used.
  - Type and magnitude of test load shall suit the structure type so strength is truly tested; apply load **slowly and incrementally with care**; avoid excessive local load causing local damage (which would become the weakest point).
  - Deflection/strain measurement locations and methods to suit the test purpose, analysis method and importance of structure.
  - Static load tests shall not be disturbed by vibration or impact. Where results are temperature-sensitive: keep temperature constant, or compare results at different temperatures, or minimise temperature effects.
  - Inspect cracks that may affect strength and durability; check **residual deformation** (การเปลี่ยนแปลงรูปร่างที่คงค้าง) both during and after test.
  - Load-test procedure may be referenced from **EIT 1008-38 (วสท. 1008-38), Standard for Reinforced Concrete Buildings by Strength Design Method**.
  - **NOTE:** No test-load magnitude, load-holding duration, deflection limit or recovery (%) criterion is specified in this standard — refer to วสท. 1008-38 (or ACI 318 Ch.27 / ACI 437) for numerical criteria.

## 13. Construction records (การบันทึกการก่อสร้าง) — PDF p.147

### 13.1 General (ทั่วไป)
- **[REQ]** The engineer or fully authorised representative shall record every stage of concrete work in sequence throughout. Records show careful, consistent attention, enable superiors to follow the work, and are valuable in disputes and for future alteration or change of use. Records **shall cover**:
  - quantities and proportions of concrete constituents;
  - mixing; placing; concrete testing; curing;
  - placing of reinforcement;
  - erection and removal of formwork; shoring/propping (การค้ำยัน);
  - erection sequence and connection of precast members;
  - significant construction loads placed on completed floors or walls;
  - general progress; programme of subsequent work;
  - deviations from the specification with reasons;
  - problems encountered with remedies;
  - special works arising during construction.
- **[REQ]** Records should be **handwritten** and completed **immediately at the end of the reporting period**.
- **[REQ]** Construction record reports shall be kept at the relevant location **during construction and for at least 2 years thereafter**.

**[REC] commentary (p.147–152):**
- Inspector's records rank second only to actual observation but are very important; type/amount depends on the engineer's organisation and type of work.
- Resident engineer normally handles: general progress reports; quantities of work done (estimated monthly); materials received and in stock; extra work; labour accounts; general inspection of plant and contractor operations; acceptance of parts of the structure and special works — some may be delegated to inspectors.
- Inspectors normally submit **daily** tabulated reports on material receipt, batching and placing; **weekly or monthly** summary reports; and keep a daily diary of general progress, oral instructions given/received, and extra or unusual work. Keep number of items for special work to a minimum but significant.
- Tabular forms simplify recording; order of items may vary, but keep clear, fast and avoid duplication.
- Field records normally made in **2 copies**: original to the engineer, copy kept at the site office; further copies printed at the engineer's office. On large projects issue a schedule listing required reports, number of copies and distribution.
- Use a definite system to identify concrete types and parts of structure: roads and canal walls by **chainage/distance**; dams by **block number or coordinates**; buildings by **floor** and structural design designation (member marks); bridges by substructure, piers, abutments, and superstructure **span numbers** with overall name.
- Multi-shift work: outgoing inspector informs relief of materials and placing locations in his report, sample serial numbers/IDs taken during the shift, special orders or changes, and unusual conditions.
- Separate plant and site inspectors each report only their own work; the **two reports must be cross-referenceable**.

- **(1) Materials records (การบันทึกเกี่ยวกับวัสดุ):**
  - Portland cement acceptance tests normally in the laboratory. Cement sample record form items: date; location; organisation; brand and type of cement; identification of shipment; quantity represented by sample; sampling method; sample size; chemical analysis; compound composition; fineness; specific gravity; water for normal consistency; setting time; soundness; tensile and compressive strength of standard mortar at specified ages; air content of mortar (if air-entraining cement); heat of hydration (if specified); weight of bagged cement; signature of person responsible for testing.
  - Aggregate reports (acceptance or mix control; some properties fairly constant so test once or at intervals) — select from: date; location; organisation; aggregate type; shipment identification; sampling method; sample size; absorption; specific gravity; unit weight; material finer than **No. 200 sieve**; clay lumps; soft fragments; organic matter; shale, coal and lignite; other deleterious substances; sieve analysis (grading); fineness modulus; maximum size; oversize and undersize; % crushed particles; petrographic composition; soundness; alkali reactivity; toughness; abrasion; strength and shrinkage of mortar/concrete to characterise aggregate; signature of person responsible.
  - Specification normally requires engineer's representative to keep contractor's records of materials received, for checking and verifying quantities used in the structure.
  - Suggested tabular form — rows per material (Portland cement, chemical admixtures, aggregates, steel, embedded items); columns:

    | Material type | Unit of measure | On hand at start of shift | Received (shift / to date) | Rejected (shift / to date) | Used (shift / to date) | Wasted (shift / to date) | On hand at end of shift |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

    Remarks space for: delivery delays, storage conditions, maximum cement storage time, damage/spoiled material, rejections.
- **(2) Mix proportion records (การบันทึกเกี่ยวกับส่วนผสม):** no general form (many proportioning methods). Tabulated calculation/record forms per criteria save field time. Records normally show given conditions and results: mix proportions; type and physical properties of cement, aggregates and mineral admixtures; type of chemical admixture; air content; yield (volume of concrete per batch).
- **(3) Batching and mixing records (การบันทึกเกี่ยวกับการวัดปริมาณวัสดุผสมและการผสม):** kept at site, summarised in daily report. Include: batch serial numbers per concrete class with start and end times of sequence; aggregate moisture content; aggregate quantities (gross weights or volumes actually batched) as shown by scale settings or other meters; batch recorder or mixer revolution counter records; number of empty cement bags returned; consistency and appearance of concrete; charging method for each batch. On large works, automatic recorders may record batch weights, mixing time and consistency, from which total materials used and total concrete produced per shift can be calculated. Also record **calibration/checking of weighing and measuring equipment**.
- **(4) Placing and curing records (การบันทึกเกี่ยวกับการเทและการบ่ม):** tabular form, rows = each part of structure cast during shift; columns (fill with dates):

    | Excavation completed / approved | Forms inspected / approved | Reinforcement & embedded items approved | Concrete placed (also note date/time of placing each part on drawing) | Initial curing started | Final curing started | Curing completed | Forms removed | Surface defects repaired | Part accepted by engineer |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **(5) Special records (การบันทึกพิเศษ):** special work (e.g. pile driving, cement grouting) recorded on similar forms. For grouting: location and depth of each hole, time intervals between changes of conditions, mix ratio, grouting pressure, quantity of cement/grout injected, and appearance of grout at other holes. For special and unit-cost work, materials and labour normally recorded separately.
- **(6) Daily report (รายงานการบันทึกประจำวัน)** — select items relevant to the job:

    | Heading | Items |
    |---|---|
    | Identification (การระบุ) | Date, shift, type and location of work, contractor's representative, inspector |
    | Materials | Type, source, quantities received, rejected, used, wasted, discarded and on hand; field samples sent to laboratory |
    | Field aggregate tests | Sieve analysis, % undersize, specific gravity, absorption, moisture content, unit weight and bulk volume (if volume batching), deleterious substances |
    | Proportioning / batching & mix (per concrete class) | Mix ratio, batch size, calculated yield, air content, mixing time, number and size of batches, total calculated volume, consistency at mixer |
    | Parts of structure prepared for placing | Excavation, piling, shoring, formwork, reinforcement, construction joints, openings, dowels/keys, embedded items |
    | Placing | Weather (temperature, humidity, wind, sky, time of observation), adequacy of crew and equipment; start and stop times of placing; delays; abnormal batches; type and volume of concrete placed; parts completed |
    | Concrete tests | Consistency before placing, unit weight, air content, temperature; strength specimens (identification, mix, curing, test age, specimens sent to lab) |
    | Curing and form removal (per part of structure) | Curing start date and age (in hours), completion date; side and soffit forms removed (with concrete age); condition of formed surfaces; repair of defective areas; making good damaged parts |
    | Special work | Grouting, pressure injection, architectural concrete |
    | Labour account | Labour, materials, plant/tools |
    | General | Conditions or changes, work organisation, equipment or methods, any unusual features |

- **(7) Summary reports (รายงานการบันทึกแบบสรุป):** weekly or monthly, compiled from daily reports, showing progress and average quality; include: material quantities; parts of structure completed; typical aggregate and concrete test results (in some cases max, min and mean); quantity of each concrete class by calculation and by measurement; repair of surface defects; repair of damaged concrete; significant changes in equipment or methods; weather conditions.
- **(8) Diary (อนุทิน):** each inspector keeps a diary as part of the engineer's construction record: progress, delays, unsatisfactory conditions, agreements and disagreements with the contractor on important matters, gist of important discussions, special instructions received, and any information not recorded elsewhere that may be future evidence in disputes on quality or payment. Brief but clear and complete; **not personal**; remember it may be read in **court**. Sketches are useful. Keep in a separate standard field book; **date every entry**; write in ink or a reasonably hard pencil (e.g. **4H**); corrections by **striking out, not erasing**.
- **(9) Photographs (ภาพถ่าย):** periodic progress photos are an important part of records. Inspector decides whether needed and approximate type/number. Progress photos taken regularly; additional photos to record e.g. excavation surface condition, incorrect placing methods or unusual construction methods.
