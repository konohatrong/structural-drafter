# 11 Connections to steel, kinks, erection (DG34 §6.1 – 6.2)

**Principle:** use **standard AISC Manual connections** wherever possible. They are familiar to everyone, adjustable and cheap. Agree the types with the fabricator and erector early. Connections to concrete and masonry → `11B_CONNECTIONS_CONCRETE_MASONRY.md`. Seismic slip details → 04B.

## Steel-to-steel connections [DG]

| Need | Connection | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Simple stringer end (shear) | Any Manual Part 10 shear connection: single plate, single angle, double angle, shear end plate, seated, tee | Figs 6-1, 6-2: a channel piece or plate at the stringer end, bolted to a tab on the W beam |
| **Adjustment** | **Extended single plate with horizontal slots** and a fit-up gap (Fig. 6-3) | For existing structures and erection tolerance |
| Hanger or axial | Double angle, end plate, extended plate (Manual Part 15) | Also struts carrying lateral load to the lateral system (10) |
| Moment | **Bolted flange plates or end plates** (no field welding); welded alternatives possible (Manual Part 12) | For a cantilevered stair or landing: a moment joint through the support (Fig. 6-5), or the stringer **continuous over the support** with stiffeners (Fig. 6-6) |
| Bracing | Manual Part 13, AISC Design Guide 29 | Fig. 6-7: single-angle brace under a landing |

## At the stair opening [DG]

- The **slab-edge angle**, typically **6 in. overhang with ± 1 in. adjustment**, carries the slab edge and sets the opening (Fig. 6-8). Fix its final position during detailing, or **ship it loose** for field welding.
- **Guard at the top landing:**
  - post welded to the side of the slab-edge angle, with a deformed-bar anchor in line with the post (Fig. 6-9); or
  - post on a slab-edge plate on top of the support beam (Fig. 6-10).
- Matching dummy members can continue the stringer line for appearance.

## Erection [DG]

- Stairs usually go in **after** the frame or core.
- Use **top-mounted bearing connections** and **erection aids**: angle struts or seats that let the crane set the flight and release it at once (Fig. 6-11). The final bolts or welds are made later.
- Bolts against a core wall may be unreachable. Choose connections that can be made from the open side.

## Kinked ("dog-leg") stringers [DG]

At the junction of the sloping and horizontal parts:

| Stringer | Joint |
|---|---|
| Plate | Two-sided PJP or CJP groove weld |
| HSS | One-sided PJP or CJP. **Backing bars inside an HSS are awkward**, so plan the weld |
| Channel | CJP. The force normal to the sloping member is resisted by **flange bending** back to the web, because the channel's axis is off the web. Usually small, but check it |
| W shape | CJP flanges and web, plus **stiffeners at the kink** if flange bending fails (Fig. 6-13) |

## Office note [REC]

For every connection, state on the drawing:
- the type (shear tab, end plate, seat);
- bolt grade, diameter and number;
- hole type, and slot direction and length;
- which end is **fixed** and which **slides**.

Show the erection aids on the erection drawing. Weld and bolt symbols follow steel S5 – S8; tag the details with the guide §9.4 callouts.
