# 02 Stair types, classes and terms (DG34 Ch. 2, after NAAMM)

**Type** = geometry. **Class** = construction quality, finish and cost. Every class must meet the same strength and safety requirements; the class changes appearance, not performance.

## Types [DG]

| Type | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Straight** | Straight stringers, slope usually < 50°. Arrangements: **straight run** (one flight, or flights in line with platforms between), **parallel** (flights side by side, reversing at a landing; 180° counts as parallel), **angled** (flights at an angle with a platform), **scissor** (two straight runs in opposite directions on either side of a divider) | By far the most common; all egress stairs here |
| Circular | Open circular plan, one centre | Feature stairs |
| Curved | Two or more centres (oval, elliptical) | Feature stairs |
| Alternating tread | Treads alternately on each side of a centre stringer, 50° – 70°, handrails both sides, descended facing outward | Maintenance access only; not egress except special cases |
| Ship ladder | Flat treads, 50° – 70°, handrails both sides | Maintenance access only; not egress except special cases |

## Classes, in order of rising cost [DG]

| Class | Typical construction |
|---|---|
| **Industrial** | Purely functional: factories, warehouses, fire escapes. Plate, channel or HSS stringers; grating or floor-plate treads and platforms; open risers; pipe, tube, angle or bar guards; hex bolts; welds not ground. Exterior: grating or perforated plate, solid surfaces sloped to drain |
| **Service** | Enclosed stairwells, secondary or egress stairs in multi-storey buildings. Industrial-type stringers; filled-pan or plate treads; exposed or open risers; pipe or bar guards; exposed soffit; hex bolts underneath; only welds in the travel area ground smooth |
| **Commercial** | Public use, open or enclosed. Exposed channel, plate or HSS stringers; steel risers; ornamental bar or HSS guards, or simple pipe; countersunk bolts where visible; conspicuous welds smooth, joints closely fitted |
| **Architectural** | Feature stairs, often custom. Low pitch (low risers, wide treads); hidden joints, smooth welds, covered soffits; special stringers, ornamental guards |

**Economy:** where function matters more than looks, specifying a lower class saves money.

## Names of the parts (DG34 Figs 2-11, 2-12) [DG]

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flight | One sloping run of treads between landings |
| Stringer | Sloping member carrying the treads. **Face stringer** on the open side, **wall stringer** against the wall |
| Tread / riser / nosing | Walking surface / vertical face / leading edge of a tread. The nosing line sets guard and handrail heights |
| Landing (platform) | Level area. **Main floor landing** at a floor, **intermediate landing** between floors |
| Flight header beam | Landing beam that receives the flight ends |
| Platform (rear) beam | Landing beam on the far side from the flights |
| Support beam / post / hanger | Building members carrying a landing from beside, below or above |
| Stringer kink | Bend where a sloping stringer meets a horizontal run (a "dog-leg"; see 11) |
| Guard | Barrier on an open side: top rail, bottom rail, posts, infill, toe plate |
| Handrail | Graspable rail on brackets, on the guard or on a wall |
| Deck span | Tread or landing span between stringers or beams |

## Office note [REC]

Use these terms on drawings, with the Thai equivalents in the general notes. Mark prefixes (ST stringer, LB landing beam) follow steel S3.
