# 13 Tolerances, galvanizing, AESS, erection (DG34 Ch. 8)

**Governing rule:** whatever the tolerance of the support structure, the **finished stair must keep its code geometry** (uniform risers within ⅜ in., minimum widths, headroom). Absorb tolerance in the **connections and gaps**, never in the riser count or the clear widths. Field-verify critical dimensions **before fabrication**.

## Tolerances to design for [DG]

| Material | Typical tolerance | Gap or allowance recommended |
|---|---|---|
| **Steel**: mill (ASTM A6), fabrication (Code of Standard Practice §6.4), erection (COSP §7.13: about **1/500** of the working-point distance) | Secondary steel positioned **± ⅜ in. (10 mm)** | **Gap ¼ – ⅜ in. (6 – 10 mm)** between the stair and the steel or stud walls; slotted holes, shim gaps, welded fit-up allowance |
| **Cast-in-place concrete** (ACI 117) | Walls and columns **¼ in. per 10 ft (6 mm / 3 m)** out of plumb; spacings ¼ in. per 10 ft, **≤ ½ in. (13 mm) per bay**; sections ≤ 12 in. +⅜ / −¼ in.; floor openings **+1 / −¼ in.**, misplaced **± ½ in.** in plan | **Gap ¼ – ½ in. (6 – 13 mm)**; adjustable connections |
| **Masonry** (ASTM C90, TMS) | Units ± ⅛ in.; walls ± ¼ in. in 10 ft, ± ⅜ in. in 20 ft, **± ½ in. maximum** plumb; storey-to-storey alignment ± ¾ in. non-bearing, ± ½ in. bearing | **Gap ¼ – ½ in.**; adjustable connections |

- AESS tolerances are tighter: COSP §10.2 – 10.6.
- The *Handbook of Construction Tolerances* (Ballast) is the general reference.
- Tolerances add up across trades. Plan the gaps at design stage, accept that some field fixes will still happen, and keep adjustment in the details: slots, shims, loose slab-edge angles, field-welded seats (07, 11).

## Galvanized stairs [DG]

For exterior or corrosive settings. They cost more to make, but less to maintain.

- **Avoid field welding.** The coating must be ground off and touched up. **Field-bolt.**
- **Bolt holes shrink** with the coating. Agree the hole size with the galvanizer. Oversized holes need **slip-critical** joints and their surface preparation.
- Use galvanized bolts, hardware and compatible weld metal (no dissimilar metals).
- Not for strongly acidic or alkaline environments, or above **400 °F (≈ 200 °C)**.
- **Kettle size** limits the size of each piece or assembly.
- **Vent and drain holes** are needed in closed sections (HSS stringers, rails).
- **Distortion** of thin or asymmetric assemblies is possible.

## Architecturally exposed steel (AESS) [DG]

Feature stairs, guards and handrails often fall under COSP §10. That means extra design, detailing, fabrication and erection effort, and close coordination with the architect on welds, joints, finishes and tolerances.

## Erectability and temporary support [DG]

- Next to a core wall, **access to bolts can be blocked**. Choose connections that can be made from the open side, or weld.
- **Masonry phasing:** the stair may need **temporary support** until the wall reaches the landing level and the pockets are grouted.
- Agree the sequence and access limits with the general contractor, architect and SER.
- Use erection aids (11) so the crane is released quickly.

## Office note [REC]

On a steel-stair set, add a general note: "VERIFY OPENING AND WALL POSITIONS ON SITE BEFORE FABRICATION; MAINTAIN RISER UNIFORMITY ⅜ in. (10 mm) PER FLIGHT". Show the gaps as dimensions with their unit. Galvanized jobs: show the vent holes and "NO FIELD WELDING".
