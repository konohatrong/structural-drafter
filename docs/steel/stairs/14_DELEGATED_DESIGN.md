# 14 Delegated design and checklists (DG34 Ch. 9, Appendix A)

**Delegated design** hands the stair design to the fabricator, usually through an SSE. It may cover everything, or only the structure, within criteria set by the architect and SER. Responsibility is often unclear; DG34 cites Pryse et al. (1996), "Metal Stairs and Railings: Will the Responsible Designer Please Step Forward?".

## Design documents must give, at least [DG]

- plans, sections and elevations at each stair;
- **clear distances** at stairs and landings, the guard layout and the handrail position;
- preferred or required member types, and the element layout;
- **slab openings**;
- **attachment details** to the building, full or conceptual;
- special requirements.

If the SER designs the connections, include the COSP §3.1 information. **The specifications must agree with the drawings**: architectural items from the architect, structural items from the SER.

## Responsibility [DG]

- Stairs, guards and handrails are **life-safety** elements. Only the architect and SER know the project's egress and code needs, so **the architect directs the geometry**.
- The architect and SER **keep responsibility for the whole structure** and confirm through submittal review that the delegated part fits it. The SSE stays responsible for the delegated design itself.

## Submittals [DG]

- The **SSE seals the calculations and the stair design drawings**; the fabricator makes the shop and erection drawings from them.
- The SSE **reviews the shop drawings**.
- **Requiring the SSE to seal the fabricator's drawings is discouraged.** Some states forbid it, and it moves no responsibility.
- The architect and SER still **review and approve** the shop and erection drawings.
- **Framework:** NAAMM stair and railing manuals, plus the AISC Code of Standard Practice.

## Appendix A checklists (condensed) [DG]

**The architect provides:**
- tread and riser number and size;
- clear widths, at flights and at landings;
- floor and landing elevations;
- finishes at treads, landings and floors;
- adjacent wall construction and finishes;
- stair class and finish quality;
- special items: refuge areas, standpipes;
- member types;
- guard and handrail types, layout, dimensions and finishes.

**The SER provides:**
- loads and deflection limits, for the stair and for the guards and handrails;
- limits on supports to the building;
- member type or size limits;
- special building details: thickened slab at the stringer base, embeds or slab edge.

**Component suppliers provide:**
- **Precast treads or planks:** weight, support forces, deflection limit, allowed connections, structural properties (or "non-structural").
- **Grating:** weight, forces, deflection limit, connections.
- **Special finishes:** weight.
- **Non-steel guards or infill:** weight, anchorage forces, deflection limit, connections.
- **Handrail brackets:** test report or approval, and the allowed connections.
