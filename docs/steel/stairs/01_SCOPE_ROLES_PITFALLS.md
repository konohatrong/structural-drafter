# 01 Scope, roles and recurring problems (DG34 Purpose, Ch. 1)

## Scope [DG]

- **Covered:** steel-framed stairs, their guards and handrails, and their connections to the building. The guide gives:
  - load determination (gravity, seismic);
  - tread and riser design;
  - stringer design (simple span, and with an integrated landing);
  - guard and handrail assembly;
  - typical connections.
- **Not covered:** custom or self-supporting stair towers, nonsteel elements (concrete, glass, aluminium), and unusual architectural designs.
- **Companion references:**
  - NAAMM *Metal Stairs Manual* (AMP 510) and *Pipe Railing Systems Manual* (AMP 521);
  - AISC Design Guide 11 for vibration.
- AISC classes stairs and handrails as "other steel items", outside the *Code of Standard Practice*. The guide still recommends designing their members and connections to AISC 360 and the Manual, and having all parties agree to the *Code of Standard Practice* as the trade framework.

## Roles [DG]

| Party | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Architect** | Egress geometry: widths, rise and run, landings, guard and handrail layout, finishes, class |
| **SER**, structural engineer of record | The building structure. Supports for the stair, loads, deflection limits, allowed connection types; reviews the stair submittal for compatibility |
| **SSE**, specialty structural engineer | Designs the stair itself, often for the fabricator under delegated design. Can be the SER |
| Detailer, fabricator, erector | Shop drawings and fit-up. Consult them early on preferred members and connections |

## The five recurring problems [DG]

1. **Shaft or opening too small.** Size it early (07) from egress width, tread and riser layout, landing depth, stringer widths, gaps, guard space and connection room. Build in adjustment: slotted holes, extended shear plates, adjustable slab-edge angles, loose slab-edge angles shipped for field welding. Survey existing or concrete and masonry openings before detailing.
2. **Code requirements unclear.** Geometry is life-safety. The architect must give exact dimensions and confirm them before detailing, and review the shop drawings.
3. **Weak or inconsistent design documents.** Typical notes and standard details that contradict the specification cause RFIs and delay. Either show the stair fully or delegate it fully and clearly. Use the checklists in 14.
4. **No structural support for the stair.** Stairs are often drawn pictorially with nothing to hang them from.
   - Every intermediate landing needs at least **two** support points, preferably **four corners**.
   - Building members must exist at landing level, or above it for hangers, or below it for posts.
   - Flights then span between the landing and the floor framing.
5. **Contract ambiguity.** Deferred submittals, delegated design and design-build each carry different expectations and liability. Settle scope before contract.

## Design philosophy [DG]

- **Layout first:** the code and occupancy fix the geometry, and the structure must suit it.
- **Comfort governs** many members: deflection and vibration, not strength.
- **Construction governs** the details:
  - erection sequence (stairs usually go in after the frame or core);
  - bolted rather than field-welded connections;
  - post-installed anchors.
- **Interfaces:** steel framing, concrete frames, cast-in-place cores, masonry cores and freestanding stairs each bring their own tolerance and fit-up problems (13).

## Office note [REC]

Before the first stair sketch, record in the design basis who is the SSE, the geometry code and the loads.
