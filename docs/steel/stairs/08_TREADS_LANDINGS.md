# 08 Treads, risers and landings (DG34 §4.1, 4.2, 4.5, 4.6)

## Tread and riser types [DG]

| Type | Design notes |
|---|---|
| **Pan tread with concrete fill**: light-gauge pan and riser | Design the pan to AISI S100 with effective sections. **The concrete is filler only: it adds weight, not strength.** Welding the pan to the stringers shortens its unbraced widths. 12 ga + 2 in. fill ≈ 30 psf |
| **Checkered plate** (ASTM A786) | AISC Manual Table 3-18a (deflection L/100) and 3-18b (24 ksi LRFD / 16 ksi ASD). Get the real grade from the supplier. A bent plate with 2 in. legs works as a small channel: Ex. 10.2.2 uses ³⁄₁₆ in. over a 3 ft span, adequate for 60 psf or 1,000 lb. Multi-span action allows thinner plate, so **state the spans** |
| **Grating treads** | Wet, outdoor and industrial areas. Standard: **1¼ × ³⁄₁₆ in. 19-W-4 serrated grating with a checkered-plate nosing**. Strength from the maker. Specify bar direction, depth, banding and nosing. Things fall through, so think about what is below |
| **Non-steel** (precast, wood, glass) | Supplier's design. Check how it interacts with the steel: wood movement, tolerances, imposed forces |

## Tread connections [DG]

- **Direct welding** of pan or plate to the stringer, as a shop-made flight assembly. Watch for **burn-through** and the low weld strength of thin material (AWS D1.1 / D1.3).
- **Carrier angle or plate** welded to the stringer, with the tread welded or bolted onto it.
- **Bolted grating treads**, usually **shop bolted** because field access is poor.
- **Stringer under the treads** (HSS or W): a built-up plate seat is needed for each tread.
- The connections can also **brace the stringer** and stiffen the flight (09, 10).

## Landing construction [DG]

From most common:
1. concrete on metal deck (also a good diaphragm);
2. concrete on stiffened plate;
3. checkered plate;
4. grating, clipped or bolted;
5. precast or other non-steel floor.

Match the building's floor system and finishes.

## Landing support [DG]

| Scheme | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Integrated landing** | Carried by the stair's own framing; the stringers continue to a support. The simplest scheme |
| **Post-supported** | Posts down to the slab or floor below; check that support (a locally thickened slab). May need bracing or a diaphragm |
| **Hanger-supported** | HSS, angle, pipe, rod or cable from above. **Brace long rods or cables** against sway. Ex. 10.1.6: L3×3×³⁄₁₆ |
| **Building supports** | Core walls: **embeds** (concrete), **beam pockets** (masonry, built around the stair with temporary bracing), or **post-installed anchors** into the finished wall. The SER details the grouted cores, bond beams and reinforcement (11B) |

Every support:
- **avoid torsion in the supporting member**: use standard shear connections, or add framing so posts and hangers bear concentrically;
- an intermediate landing needs **≥ 2 supports, ideally 4 corners** (01);
- fit-up minimum for posts and hangers: **≥ 3 in. wide, ≥ ³⁄₁₆ in. thick** (Ex. 10.1.5: HSS3×3×³⁄₁₆);
- in cores, stairs may **ship in pieces** to absorb wall tolerances, at the cost of erection time.
