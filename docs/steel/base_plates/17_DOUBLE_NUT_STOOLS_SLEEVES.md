# 17 Double-nut joints, stools and sleeves (DG1 App. A1, A1.1)

## Special joints [DG]

| Joint | Use and point to note |
|---|---|
| **Double-nut** (no grout; levelling nut under, top nut over the plate) | Light and sign poles, signal masts. **Very stiff and reliable** for moment. The rods carry **all** load: tension, compression and shear; the foundation must take it from the rods. Non-redundant and wind-fatigue loaded, so special tightening and inspection (18). Pretension improves fatigue life and load sharing |
| **Sleeved rods** | Machinery bases needing close alignment: an oversized sleeve lets the rod flex to fit. The bottom anchorage spans the sleeve |
| **Pretensioned rods** | Vibration or cyclic loads. **Pretension holds only against steel**: concrete creep and shrinkage of 0.05 in. (1.3 mm) relieves all pretension in a 24 in. (600 mm) Gr 55 rod. Use a **steel sleeve** to carry the pretension from the anchor plate to the base plate (Fig. A1.1) |
| **Stool** (Fig. A1.2) | Large mill columns with big base moments. The base plate is set early with oversized holes; **fillet-welded stools** avoid CJP welds to a heavy plate (over 2 in. thick these need special toughness); long rods adjust easily to the stool cap holes |

## Rods in compression (A1.1) [DG]

- Short rods in double-nut joints: **Rc = Fy Ag**, φ 0.90, Ω 1.67.
- Clear gap under the plate usually **≤ 2.5 in. (64 mm)**. If the distance from the levelling nut to the concrete exceeds **4 rod diameters**, check buckling as a column.
- Headed rods push on their head; deformed bars along their length. Check the concrete to ACI.

Fatigue design, redundancy and pole plate thickness: 17B. Installation and inspection: 18, 18B.
