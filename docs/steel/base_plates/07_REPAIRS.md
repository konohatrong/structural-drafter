# 07 Anchor rod repairs (DG1 §2.11)

## General [DG + CODE]

- **OSHA: any rod modification during construction must be approved by the EOR.**
- The EOR weighs the repair against replacing part of the foundation. **Keep records**; the EOR may require inspection or testing.
- Most repairs are standard and need no calculation.

## Rods in the wrong position (§2.11.1) [DG]

Ask: one rod or the whole pattern? How far out? What strength must the rods have? When was it found?

| Situation | Repair |
|---|---|
| Found **before the plate is made** | Change the pattern or the plate. If rods hit the shaft, cut and reinforce the flange or web |
| **One or two rods** off, column already made | **Slot the plate** and cover the slot with a **plate washer** |
| **Whole pattern** off uniformly | Cut the plate off and **re-weld it offset**; check the plate for the eccentricity. Turn it over if needed for a clean welding face |
| Rods more than **a couple of inches (≈ 50 mm)** off | Cut them off and install **drilled-in adhesive rods** to the maker's instructions, with code inspection. Avoid the bars; if a bar is cut, check the foundation |

## Bent or not vertical (§2.11.2) [DG]

- Secure rods in the template and keep them clear of bars so they set **plumb**.
- **Flag rod locations** so equipment does not hit them (DG1 shows rods flattened by a crane under snow).
- F1554 allows hot and cold bending, but bending in the threads is a problem.
- Field bending: **Gr 36 only, ≤ 45°**. Cold-bend up to about **1 in. (25 mm)**; larger rods may be heated to **1,200 °F (650 °C)**. Use a **hickey** bar. Inspect for cracks after bending; load-test if the tension strength is in doubt.

## Projection too short or too long (§2.11.3) [DG]

- First check that the **right rods** were installed: a short rod may go through the bottom of the foundation; a long one may lack embedment.
- **Partly engaged nut:** strength in proportion to threads engaged, if **at least half** the nut's threads are engaged. **Do not weld the nut to the rod** (not prequalified).
- Rods for erection only: drill a new hole and add a **drilled-in rod**.
- Rods in tension:
  - **Coupling nut** (Fig. 2.4, Table 2.4) with an enlarged hole and oversize shims;
  - **Weld on a threaded extension**: Gr 36, or Gr 55 with S1. Butt-weld with a **run-out tab** (Fig. 2.5a), or with **splice bars** (Fig. 2.5b). Both can be full strength. See AISC DG21.
- Rods too long: add **plate washers** to reach the thread. This is why rods carry **3 in. (75 mm) extra thread** (04).

## Pattern rotated 90° (§2.11.4) [DG]

Very hard to fix. Sometimes the plate can be removed and rotated; usually the rods are **cut off and drilled-in rods** installed. [REC] Prefer symmetric patterns so this cannot happen (04).

## Office note [REC]

Add to the general notes: "ANY ANCHOR ROD OUT OF TOLERANCE, BENT, CUT OR EXTENDED SHALL BE REPORTED TO THE ENGINEER. NO FIELD BENDING, HEATING OR WELDING WITHOUT WRITTEN APPROVAL."
