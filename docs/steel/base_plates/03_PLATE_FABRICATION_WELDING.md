# 03 Plate size, fabrication and welding (DG1 §2.2 – §2.4)

## Shape and minimum thickness [DG]

- Most plates are **square**, to match square footings and square rod patterns. Exceptions: moment bases and columns next to walls.
- Common office minimums:
  - posts and light HSS columns: **½ in. (13 mm)**;
  - other columns: **¾ in. (19 mm)**.

## Cutting [DG + CODE]

- Plates are usually **thermally cut**. Rod and grout holes are drilled or thermally cut.
- AISC M2.2: thermally cut edges under calculated tension must be free of round-bottom gouges deeper than 3/16 in. (5 mm) and of sharp V-notches; deeper gouges and notches are ground and weld-repaired.
- Plate perimeter edges are not in tension, so this is a workmanship guide there. **Rod holes may be in tension and must meet it.**

## Finishing for bearing (AISC M2.8) [CODE]

| Thickness | Requirement |
|---|---|
| ≤ 2 in. (50 mm) | No milling, if contact bearing is satisfactory |
| 2 – 4 in. (50 – 100 mm) | Straighten by pressing, or mill |
| > 4 in. (100 mm) | Mill the bearing surfaces |

- Not needed: the **bottom** surface when the plate is grouted; the **top** when the column is CJP-welded to the plate.
- AISC M4.4: a gap up to **1/16 in. (1.6 mm)** is acceptable. From 1/16 to ¼ in. (6 mm), if an investigation shows too little contact, pack with **non-tapered mild steel shims**.
- So plates over 4 in. need not be milled if they are flat enough under the column.
- **Order plates over about 3 in. (75 mm) with ¼ – ½ in. (6 – 13 mm) extra** thickness for milling. Usually only the area under the column shaft is milled; the column is then set from the bottom of the shaft.

## Column-to-plate welds [DG]

- Welds are often sized to **develop the anchor rods in tension**. Example: a 5/16 in., 2½ in. long fillet on each flange develops a 1 in. F1554 Gr 36 rod (with the transverse-load increase).
- **Fillet welds rather than groove welds**, except for large moment bases.
- **Avoid the weld-all-around symbol**, especially on W shapes: the flange-toe and fillet-radius welds add little and cost much.
- **W column, compression only:** 5/16 in. fillets on **one side of each flange** (Fig. 2.1) are usually enough. For moment or tension, add fillets on all faces up to ¾ in. before going to groove welds.
- **Rectangular HSS, compression only:** weld the four flats and skip the corners (no out-of-position welding). **With moment or tension and corner rods, weld the corners**: the yield line forms there.
- Minimum fillet size depends on the **thinner** part joined (AISC 360-05 onward).

## Shop or loose plates [DG]

- Most plates are **shop-welded** to the column; erectors prefer it.
- Heavy plates for tall buildings were once shipped **loose**, set on three levelling screws (Fig. 2.2) and grouted before the shaft. Still used where weight or crane capacity demands it (06).

## Office note [REC]

On the base plate detail, show the weld size and the faces welded (no all-round symbol on W columns), the plate grade, size and thickness, and the milling requirement for plates over 50 mm.
