# 06 Column erection and grouting (DG1 §2.9, §2.10)

## Before erection [DG + CODE]

- **OSHA:** the general contractor notifies the erector **in writing** that the rods are ready: layout checked, repairs made, concrete at required strength. The erector rechecks and sets elevations.
- In every method, **tighten all rod nuts before the crane releases the column**. This is not pretension, only enough to stop the base moving. If nuts are loosened to plumb, **brace the column** meanwhile.

## Setting methods [DG]

| Method | How | Use and cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting nut and washer** (§2.9.1) | Levelling nut and washer under the plate on each of four rods | Easy, cheap, stable with rods outside the column. Rods are then **in compression**: check **push-out at the footing bottom**. Limit to **lightly loaded** columns during erection; the nut still carries load after grouting |
| **Setting (levelling) plate** (§2.9.2) | Plate about ¼ in. (6 mm) thick, slightly larger than the base plate, max. about 24 in. (600 mm) (warping); set on grout with jam nuts or shims, tapped to level | Most stable base; positive check of rods before erection. Holes 1/16 in. over rod if also the template. Good in **excavations** where water and soil wash under the plate. Gaps judged by AISC M4.4 |
| **Shim stacks** (§2.9.3) | Steel shim packs about 4 in. (100 mm) wide at the four edges | All compression goes to the foundation **without the rods**; carries large dead load before grouting |
| **Large plates set first** (§2.9.4) | Plate set and grouted before the column, on wedge shims or **levelling screws** (sleeve nuts welded to the plate edge, Fig. 2.2) | When weight or crane capacity demands. Table 2.3 holes. Give the shaft **stools or erection aids**; avoid field welding (preheat of a heavy plate) |

## Grout [DG]

- **Strength at least twice the foundation concrete.** Bearing calculations use the concrete f′c.
- **Space:**
  - on a finished floor: about **1 in. (25 mm)**;
  - on a footing or pier: **1½ – 2 in. (38 – 50 mm)**;
  - more for large plates and plates with shear lugs.
- **Grout holes:** not needed for most plates. Plates up to **24 in. (600 mm)** wide: form round the edge and pour from one side until grout flows out the other. **Larger plates or plates with lugs:** one or two holes, **2 – 3 in. (50 – 75 mm)**, with a form and a filling head to push the grout to all edges.
- Follow the manufacturer for mixing, curing and **cold-weather protection**.
- **Non-shrink, flowable** grout; grout pockets for lugs big enough to fill (14).

## Responsibility [DG]

- Grout belongs to the **concrete contractor**, not in the steel section.
- Require **prompt grouting when the erector reports a column in its final position**.

## Office note [REC]

- Steel S8.4 uses a levelling nut on every rod and grouts after plumbing and bracing; that is the setting-nut method above. For heavy columns during erection, call for shim stacks or a setting plate instead, and check rod push-out.
- General note: "NON-SHRINK GROUT, f′c ≥ 2 × FOUNDATION CONCRETE, 25 – 50 mm THICK, PLACED BY THE CONCRETE CONTRACTOR".
